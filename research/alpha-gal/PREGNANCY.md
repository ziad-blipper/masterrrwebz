## 1. Is the baby OK?

**For the reactions she has actually been having — yes, and the reassurance is mechanistic, not just
hopeful.**

- **IgE does not cross the placenta.** The baby is not having an allergic reaction.
- **Fetal harm in maternal allergy is haemodynamic, not immunological.** It happens through **maternal
  hypotension and low oxygen** reducing blood flow to the placenta — not through the allergen.
- Published position: *"Available data do not suggest any direct deleterious effect of urticaria per
  se on the fetus; fetal risk is essentially linked to **severe maternal anaphylaxis**."*
- With no drop in blood pressure and no hypoxia, **there is no pathway to fetal harm.**

**The numbers, for scale:**

| | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Anaphylaxis in pregnancy | **1.6 per 100,000 maternities** (UK); 1.5–3.8 pooled |
| Maternal mortality from it | **0.09 per 100,000 live births** (UK 2012–14) |
| UKOSS series, 37 confirmed cases | 2 maternal deaths · **no infant deaths** · 41% NICU admission among those whose mother reacted before delivery |

⚠️ **Honest caveats, both worth stating:**

1. **Her reactions are not reliably mild.** Itchy mouth → **presyncope** → nausea, within minutes, is
   three organ systems including a cardiovascular sign. Presyncope is a soft marker of exactly the
   hypotension that matters. "Mild reactions don't hurt the baby" is true; "her reactions are mild"
   is not established.
2. **The pregnancy anaphylaxis literature is mostly not about her situation.** 74% of published cases
   are **peripartum** — at caesarean, triggered by antibiotics, latex, oxytocin and anaesthetic
   agents. Antenatal food-triggered anaphylaxis in an ambulatory woman is a different and much
   less-studied scenario.

**Target for anyone treating her: keep systolic BP ≥ 90 mmHg.** That is the only quantitative figure
the guidelines give, and it is a floor, not a safety threshold.

## 2. ⭐ Epinephrine — pregnancy makes it more urgent, not risky

**This is the single most important item in this document, and it has been outstanding for months.**

**ASCIA, *Acute Management of Anaphylaxis in Pregnancy*, 2024** — the best single document to hand
the OB:

> *"Adrenaline should be the first line treatment for anaphylaxis in pregnancy… **adrenaline should
> not be withheld due to a fear of causing reduced placental perfusion.**"*
>
> *"There is **no robust evidence of harm** from a dose of 0.5mg adrenaline IM for treatment of
> anaphylaxis in pregnancy."*
>
> *"Management of anaphylaxis in pregnant women is **the same as for non-pregnant women**. The
> benefits of maintaining adequate maternal blood pressure… **outweigh the possible risks of the
> brief reduction in uterine blood flow.**"*

**EAACI 2021** and **WAO 2020** say the same: dose identical in pregnancy, 0.5 mg IM mid-outer thigh,
repeatable every 5 minutes.

**The concern about uterine vasoconstriction is pharmacological theory. No source documents fetal
harm from therapeutic IM epinephrine given for anaphylaxis.** The harm in the literature comes from
**delay** — there is a 2026 review whose title names *"epinephrine hesitancy"* as a cause of
preventable morbidity and death.

**No contraindication to auto-injectors in pregnancy.** Standing advice is that at-risk pregnant
women carry **two** and have a written action plan. **Any prescriber can do this today.**

## 3. ⚠️ Correction — the positioning advice in `toolkit.md` is wrong after 20 weeks

I wrote *"lie flat, legs elevated."* That is correct in early pregnancy and **wrong later**.

**From ~20 weeks** — or when the fundus reaches the navel — lying on her back lets the uterus
compress the vena cava. **Supine can cut cardiac output by 25–30%**, which is precisely the
hypotension that threatens the baby.

| Situation | Position |
| --- | --- |
| **Under 20 weeks** | Flat with **legs elevated** — unchanged |
| **20 weeks or more**, conscious | ⭐ **Full left lateral** (left side). Legs can still be raised within that position |
| 20 weeks or more, needs to be flat (airway, CPR) | Supine **plus manual left uterine displacement** — push the uterus to her left. Preferred over tilt, because tilt degrades chest compressions |
| **Any stage** | **Never let her sit up, stand or walk** during a reaction |

## 4. ⭐ The free experiment pregnancy is about to run on her "histamine problem"

This is the most interesting finding in the whole document.

**The placenta produces diamine oxidase — the enzyme that breaks down histamine — and it rises
several hundred-fold during pregnancy.**

- Produced by extravillous trophoblasts from around **week 7**
- Climbs through months 2–3, **peaks at months 5–7**, stays there until birth
- DAO is **the only extracellular enzyme in humans that inactivates histamine**
- In non-pregnant people, serum DAO sits near the limit of detection

**So pregnancy is a natural, high-dose DAO experiment, and it costs nothing to read:**

> **If her symptoms are genuinely histamine intolerance from low DAO, they should measurably improve
> from the second trimester onward.**
>
> **If they persist or worsen through weeks 20–30 — when placental DAO is at its peak — the histamine
> label is very unlikely to be the right explanation**, and the diagnostic effort should go to
> celiac, her confirmed IgE allergy, and the never-investigated ten-year GI history instead.

**All it requires is a symptom diary through the second trimester.** A ten-year-old untested label,
settled for free.

*(Confidence: the DAO physiology is well sourced. The diagnostic inference is reasoning, not a
sourced recommendation — but it costs nothing to check.)*

## 5. ⭐ Celiac — and the finding that makes it urgent

Two independent reasons this test cannot wait: **untreated celiac carries real obstetric risk**, and
**she is currently eating gluten**, which is a prerequisite for a valid test and a state she may not
stay in.

**Meta-analysis, 18 studies:**

| Outcome | Relative risk |
| --- | --- |
| Fetal growth restriction | **1.68** (1.34–2.10) |
| Stillbirth | **1.57** (1.17–2.10) |
| Spontaneous abortion | **1.35** (1.10–1.65) |
| Preterm delivery | **1.29** (1.12–1.49) |
| Mean birthweight | **−176 g** (−266 to −86) |

A second, independent meta-analysis agrees closely.

> ### The decisive finding
>
> **Untreated celiac: preterm birth OR 2.50. Diagnosed and treated: OR 0.80 — no excess at all.**
>
> *"Only **undiagnosed** celiac disease increased risk for fetal growth restriction, stillbirth,
> preterm delivery and lower mean birthweight, whereas **early diagnosis was not linked to any
> adverse pregnancy outcomes.**"*

**The risk is not celiac. The risk is not knowing.** That is as clean an argument for a blood test as
exists anywhere in medicine.

Her ten years of abdominal pain, nausea, loose stool, headache and joint pain, with multiple food
reactions and **no ferritin ever measured**, is a textbook untested-celiac presentation. It may well
be negative. It is indefensible not to have looked.

**Order tTG-IgA + total serum IgA this week. She must keep eating gluten until it is drawn.**

## 6. ⚠️ Nutrition — the second-largest risk in this case

A year with **no mammalian meat and no dairy**, plus egg reactions, now pregnant. That removes the
principal sources of **haem iron, B12, iodine, calcium and choline** at the moment demand rises.

### The two nobody will think of

**⭐ Iodine.** *One cup of milk ≈ 85 µg ≈ **57% of a pregnant woman's daily requirement**.* She cut
dairy. Requirement is **250 µg/day** (up from 150). Deficiency impairs fetal neurological
development — iodine-deficient populations show a mean **12–13.5 IQ point reduction**.
**⚠️ Check her prenatal actually contains iodine. Many do not.**

**⭐ Choline.** Recommended **450 mg/day**; US average among pregnant women is **319 mg**, and **only
8.5% meet the target**. **Most prenatals contain 0–50 mg — many contain none.** One egg yolk supplies
**147 mg**. **She avoids meat, dairy *and* egg — the intersection of every risk factor.** A dedicated
choline supplement deserves active consideration.

### The rest

| Nutrient | Target / threshold | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Iron** | **Ferritin <30 µg/L = deficient in pregnancy** — higher than the usual cutoff, and it will not be ordered by default | No mammalian meat = **no haem iron at all.** Deficiency in the first two trimesters raises low birthweight; in the third, long-term neurocognitive deficits. Iron drives myelination and neurotransmitter synthesis |
| **B12** | >300 ng/L; prefer **holotranscobalamin**, which is unaffected by pregnancy | **<250 ng/L → 2.5–3× neural tube defect risk.** Fetal accumulation sets the newborn's stores — a breastfed infant can become deficient even if the mother has no symptoms |
| **Vitamin D** | ≥50 nmol/L; 1000 IU/day maintenance | Dairy elimination removes fortified sources |
| **Calcium** | **1000 mg/day** | Non-dairy: fortified plant milks, calcium-set tofu, tinned fish with bones, dark greens |
| **DHA / omega-3** | **500–1000 mg/day including ≥500 mg DHA, from 12 weeks** | Cochrane, **70 RCTs, 19,927 women: 11% reduction in preterm birth <37 weeks, 42% reduction <34 weeks** |

### ⭐ And the finding that should change her diet in the *other* direction

**Maternal elimination diets during pregnancy do not prevent allergy in the child — and they carry
real harm.**

- **Cochrane:** *"Prescription of an antigen avoidance diet to a high-risk woman during pregnancy is
  unlikely to reduce substantially her child's risk of atopic diseases, and **such a diet may
  adversely affect maternal and/or fetal nutrition.**"*
- *"There is **no role** for maternal dietary modifications during pregnancy or lactation… as a means
  of infant allergy prevention"* (*Lancet Child Adolesc Health* 2023).
- International guidelines **unanimously discourage** avoidance diets in pregnancy.

**So every exclusion beyond her genuine IgE reactions now needs to justify itself against a
documented harm.**

> **And dairy may not even be required by her diagnosis.** Her allergy is to **serum albumin**.
> Bovine serum albumin (Bos d 6) is a **minor and heat-labile** milk component. Whether she actually
> needs to avoid dairy is a **testable question, not a given** — and **reinstating dairy would solve
> the iodine and calcium problems at a stroke.**
>
> Put this to the allergist directly. It may be the highest-value question of the appointment.

## 7. ⚠️ Medications — what changes

| | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **Amitriptyline 10 mg** *(I recommended this)* | **Hold pending OB.** No clear teratogenic signal, but a gestational-diabetes signal exists (aOR 1.52) — **at antidepressant doses of 75–150 mg, with no dose-response data for 10 mg.** Regardless: the indication is not urgent, so it is an elective exposure. Non-drug options for migraine and sleep first |
| **Colesevelam** *(I mentioned a trial)* | ⛔ **Drop it entirely.** An unproven trial for an unproven hypothesis, in a woman who has never had a basic GI workup, using a drug that **binds fat-soluble vitamins including K** — in someone already nutritionally at risk. Wrong trade. Postpartum |
| **Antihistamines** | **Cetirizine or loratadine** — best second-generation data. Class meta-analysis: no increased malformation risk (OR 1.07, 0.98–1.16). ⚠️ **They do not treat anaphylaxis and must never delay epinephrine** |
| **Famotidine** (H2) | Reasonable. >84,000-infant cohort showed no increased malformation risk |
| **NSAIDs** | **From ~20 weeks:** risk of low amniotic fluid. **From ~30 weeks: avoid** — premature closure of the ductus arteriosus (FDA, Oct 2020) |
| **Corticosteroids** | Acceptable short-course for a real indication. **Not first- or second-line for anaphylaxis** and never a substitute for epinephrine |
| ⚠️ **The supplement list** | **This is itself a medication-safety problem.** High-dose vitamin A and several herbals are teratogenic or uterotonic. **A written inventory needs OB review this week** |

## 8. Testing — what happens now, what waits

| Now (all safe) | Deferred |
| --- | --- |
| All specific IgE blood tests | ⛔ **Oral food challenge — absolute contraindication in pregnancy** (BSACI). No pork, beef, lamb, egg or fish challenge until after delivery |
| tTG-IgA + total IgA | **Skin prick testing** — a *relative* contraindication. Unnecessary here anyway, since blood tests answer the same questions. **So the raw-vs-cooked beef prick test comes off the list** |
| Baseline tryptase ⚠️ **no validated pregnancy reference range exists — repeat postpartum before interpreting** | **Endoscopy** — strong indication only, second trimester preferred, obstetric consult first. Ten years of stable symptoms is not a strong indication *unless celiac serology is positive* |
| Ferritin, B12, vitamin D, FBC | |
| Component panel: **Sus s 1, Fel d 2, Bos d 6**, cat, egg, fish, shellfish, **Anisakis** | |

**On interpretation:** total IgE does **not** vary significantly across pregnancy. Plasma volume
rises ~42–48%, which would bias late-pregnancy results *downward* — so her existing numbers stand,
and if anything under-state things. **Her diagnosis is not in doubt.**

## 9. ⭐ The risk window nobody has mentioned: delivery itself

**74% of published pregnancy anaphylaxis is peripartum** — overwhelmingly at caesarean, triggered by
**beta-lactam antibiotics, latex, oxytocin, chlorhexidine and anaesthetic agents.**

Her food allergy does not predict any of those. But her atopic status warrants:

- A **documented peripartum allergy plan** in the notes
- An **anaesthetic pre-assessment** before delivery
- **Latex-aware precautions**

**The delivery room is statistically the riskiest place in this pregnancy for an allergic event, and
it is entirely plannable.**
