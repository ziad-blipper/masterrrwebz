**Branzino (sea bass) · prawns · halibut. Symptoms ongoing at 48 hours.**

*Research synthesis for her clinicians. Not a diagnosis.*

---

## ⚠️ Correction: "two days is the wrong shape for an allergy" was half wrong

I said this twice with confidence. Half of it holds, half of it does not.

**What holds:** a *continuous* 48-hour IgE reaction does not happen. Biphasic anaphylaxis is a
**recurrence after a symptom-free gap** (mean 8.1 h, median 11 h), not two unbroken days. Protracted
anaphylaxis exists but those patients are in intensive care, not at home.

**What I got wrong — and it dissolves the whole puzzle:** **her reactions end in migraine.** By
ICHD-3, a migraine attack runs **4–72 hours untreated**, with a **postdrome of up to 48 hours** on
top.

> So: minutes-onset itchy mouth → dizziness → nausea → **migraine lasting up to three days, plus two
> more days of postdrome** is a completely ordinary shape. **There is no anomaly to explain.**

This should be read as reassuring. It removes the need for an exotic explanation.

### ⭐ The one question that settles it

**Did the itchy mouth and throat stop within an hour or two, while the headache, nausea and malaise
carried on?**

- **Yes** → an allergic reaction happened at minute zero, and the 48-hour tail is the migraine. No
  puzzle.
- **The mouth itch is still going now** → something is still actively driving it, and that is a
  different and more concerning situation.

**Split the event into two questions and it stops being confusing:**
**(A)** Was there an acute reaction at the meal? **(B)** What is generating the tail? Forcing one
diagnosis onto both is the trap.

## ⏳ The test window that is open RIGHT NOW and closes when she gets better

**Acute tryptase is gone.** Half-life 1.5–2.5 hours, back to baseline by ~24 h. ⚠️ **A normal tryptase
today says nothing about what happened 48 hours ago — do not let anyone use it to rule out
anaphylaxis.**

**But urinary mediators reflect the collection period, not a past event — and she is still
symptomatic.**

| Test | Where | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **24-hour urinary N-methylhistamine** | Mayo **NMH24** · ARUP **2011034** · Quest **39559** | ⭐ Start today |
| **11β-prostaglandin F2α** (± leukotriene E4) | Mayo **MCMRU** | Same collection |
| **Serum tryptase, interim** | any lab | Confounded by ongoing symptoms — **informative only if elevated.** Diarise a clean baseline ≥2 weeks after she is fully well |

⚠️ **Collection conditions matter:** N-methylhistamine reflects mast cells, basophils **and dietary
histamine.** She must eat **low-histamine** during the collection, and the container must be kept
**chilled**.

**This is the first time in ten years the "histamine problem" label has had a live window attached to
it.**

## The butter hypothesis: elegant, unproven, and free to falsify

I proposed this as the unifying answer. **Dedicated searching found no evidence for it**, and one
study cuts against it.

**For it:** butter contains milk protein (~0.85 g/100 g) and is not dairy-free; bovine serum albumin
is present in the milk serum phase and in residual milk-fat-globule material; heated albumin remains
allergenic in a substantial minority (**4 of 10 beef-allergic children had positive double-blind
challenges to *heated* albumin**).

**Against it:** ⚠️ **No published measurement of BSA content in butter. No measurement of intact BSA
surviving pan-heating. And no case report anywhere of butter triggering a serum-albumin-allergic
patient** — searched for specifically, nothing found. Worse: in a study of 15 patients with cat
albumin IgE, **7 had bovine albumin IgE and all 15 ate milk, pork and beef with no symptoms.**

> ### ⭐ The free test that settles it
>
> **Does she eat butter at home without trouble?** Butter on toast, buttered vegetables, croissants,
> cream in coffee.
>
> **A clean history of tolerating butter at home kills this hypothesis outright** — no lab required.

*(Clarified butter and ghee are genuinely different — casein and whey largely removed. But
restaurants use butter, not ghee.)*

**Probability it was the acute trigger: ~15–20%.** Worth keeping high on the list precisely because
it is so cheap to disprove.

## What the seafood itself could have been

### ⚠️ Scombroid is less likely than I suggested

It needs high **free histidine** in the muscle. Scombroid species run **210–1,500 mg/100 g**;
**white-fleshed fish like sea bass and halibut run under 5 mg/100 g.** Both her fish were white.

**~3%**, not the live possibility I implied. *(Verification gap: the FDA's authoritative species
table could not be retrieved — the low-histidine argument stands on separate evidence.)*

### Fish — and a nuance that matters for her specifically

**Parvalbumin, the main fish allergen, is heat-stable** — it unfolds on heating and **refolds as it
cools**. Cooking reduces it but does not abolish it.

That is the **opposite** of her established pattern. A cooked-fish reaction via parvalbumin would be
a **mechanically different, second allergy**, not an extension of the albumin story.

⭐ **But two fish allergens are heat-LABILE and do fit her phenotype: enolase and aldolase**, with
specific IgE in **62% and 50%** of fish-allergic patients respectively. Worth raising with the
allergist — though testing availability is limited.

### Prawns

**Tropomyosin (Pen a 1) is heat-stable too** — also refolds on cooling. Requires a new sensitization
outside her known pattern.

⭐ **Pen m 2 (arginine kinase) is the heat-labile shrimp allergen** and is the one that fits her.
Pen m 2 and Pen m 4 reactivity was associated with **severe** shrimp allergy in 6 of 8 cases.

Also worth knowing: shrimp tropomyosin shares **80–82% identity with dust mite tropomyosin**, so
shrimp positivity is often **mite-driven and clinically silent.** Order **Der p 10** alongside, or a
positive shrimp result will be over-read.

### ⭐ Sulphites on the prawns — the exposure nobody asks about

**Sodium metabisulphite is the standard industry anti-melanosis dip for shrimp** — typically a 2% dip
for 10 minutes, and residual SO₂ frequently exceeds guideline levels. Documented adverse effects
include nausea and gastric irritation, and reactions in sensitive individuals.

**This is a non-IgE trigger present in restaurant prawns by default. ~10%.** Ask whether prawn dishes
specifically have been a recurring problem for her.

### Anisakis

A parasite in marine fish. Heat-stable components (**Ani s 4**) mean **cooking and freezing kill the
parasite and prevent infection but do not reliably prevent allergic reactions** in someone
sensitized.

**~5%, dropping to ≤2% if the branzino was farmed and there was no severe epigastric pain.**

⚠️ **Testing trap: Ani s 3 IS tropomyosin** and cross-reacts with shrimp and dust mite. After a prawn
meal an isolated Ani s 3 positive is **uninterpretable**. Insist on **Ani s 1 and Ani s 4**. And note
background sensitization is high — 0.4–27% of asymptomatic people — so a positive p4 alone proves
nothing.

## ⭐ The question that could collapse the whole attribution

> **"In a typical month, how many two-day episodes like this does she have *without* any identifiable
> food trigger?"**

If the answer is "several," then a flare beginning near a memorable restaurant meal is **coincidence
plus recall**, and the meal explains nothing. Ten years of unexplained symptoms makes this a live
possibility, and it is the cheapest question here.

Related: **a large, high-fat restaurant meal is itself a potent trigger** in disorders of gut–brain
interaction. Dietary lipid is the most potent dietary trigger of dyspeptic symptoms, and **40–70% of
functional dyspepsia patients report symptom onset within minutes of eating.**

## Do now

1. 🚨 **Epinephrine — two auto-injectors, today.** Still the highest-value item in the whole file and
   still overdue. Do not wait for the allergist.
2. ⏳ **Start the 24-hour urine collection today** — low-histamine diet during it, container chilled.
   This window closes when she recovers.
3. 📞 **Call the restaurant today** — the four questions above.
4. **Ask whether anyone else at the table got sick**, and on what lag. This single fact separates
   food poisoning and scombroid from a personal reaction better than any blood test.
5. **Ask the butter question** — does she tolerate butter at home?
6. **Write the timeline down now**: minutes from first bite to first symptom; which symptom came
   first; **whether and when the mouth itch resolved**; what has been continuous versus intermittent.
7. **Trial a non-sedating antihistamine** and record whether it helps. Diagnostically informative —
   **but not a substitute for epinephrine.**
8. **Stool PCR panel** if there is diarrhoea or fever.

## 🚨 Go in now, don't wait, if any of these

Throat tightness, voice change, difficulty swallowing or breathing, wheeze, tongue or lip swelling ·
faintness on standing, pallor, palpitations, chest pain · new or spreading hives or swelling ·
persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down · fever, bloody diarrhoea, or severe localised
abdominal pain · worst-ever headache or any new neurological sign · **anything that is escalating
rather than settling.**

## Add to the allergist list

**Fish:** whole sea bass and halibut sIgE + parvalbumin (**Gad m 1** / **Cyp c 1**); ask about enolase
and aldolase availability.
**Shrimp:** whole shrimp + **Pen m 1**, ⭐ **Pen m 2**, **Pen m 4**, plus **Der p 10** and whole dust
mite — without the mite tests a shrimp positive cannot be interpreted.
**Anisakis:** p4 + **Ani s 1 / Ani s 4** (not Ani s 3 alone), repeated at ~4 weeks.
**CCD marker (MUXF3)** — run before interpreting any plant or invertebrate result.
**Total IgE** — mandatory context for every value above.
**Supervised challenge to butter** — the only way to settle that hypothesis properly.
