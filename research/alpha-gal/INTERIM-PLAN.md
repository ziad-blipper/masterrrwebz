Everything else in this directory is diagnostic. This is about the next few months, while
appointments and results are pending.

**The organizing rule: relief that does not erase diagnostic information is fine. Anything that
changes what a test would show has to be sequenced.**

*Research synthesis for discussion with treating clinicians. Not medical advice. Every drug named
needs a prescriber who has her full medication list.*

## 1. What must not be done

### The irreversible one

**Do not start a gluten-free diet before celiac serology is drawn.** tTG-IgA normalizes in roughly
80% of true celiacs within 6–12 months off gluten, and sometimes much faster. Recovering the
diagnosis afterwards means a formal gluten challenge — ≥3 g/day for 2–8 weeks, deliberately making
herself ill.

- **Eating gluten now?** Draw **tTG-IgA + total serum IgA** this week. Total IgA is mandatory —
  IgA deficiency causes false negatives and is over-represented in exactly this population.
- **Already off gluten?** **HLA-DQ2/DQ8** is unaffected by diet, ever. Negative result ≈ 99%
  negative predictive value and the question closes permanently.

### The one that gives her more freedom than expected

**Antihistamines suppress skin tests. They do not affect serum IgE, component testing, total IgE, or
tryptase.**

**She can take antihistamines today and have all her bloodwork drawn tomorrow.** Only the skin-testing
visit requires a hold.

| Agent | Hold before **skin testing** only |
| --- | --- |
| 2nd-gen H1 (cetirizine, fexofenadine, levocetirizine) | 3–7 days |
| Ketotifen | 5–7 days |
| **TCAs — amitriptyline, nortriptyline** | **7–14 days** |
| **Cyproheptadine** | **up to 11 days** — the worst offender |
| H2 blockers (famotidine) | 24–48 hours |
| Montelukast, oral cromolyn | **no hold needed** |
| Omalizumab | suppresses for **months** — do not start before testing |

**Baseline tryptase** must be drawn ≥24 h after any reaction resolves. Antihistamines and cromolyn do
not affect it. **Draw it now.** If >8 ng/mL, the next test is TPSAB1 for hereditary alpha-tryptasemia.

### Get the component panel drawn soon

Specific IgE **declines with prolonged avoidance.** Her pork 14.40 was measured after a year of meat
avoidance and is drifting down. The longer she waits, the muddier interpretation gets — and a falling
number must **not** be read as loss of allergy.

### Never

Home food challenges · IgG/IgG4 food sensitivity panels · **porcine DAO supplements** (§4) · serum DAO
testing · gluten-free before celiac closes · omalizumab before baseline tryptase · starting more than
one new thing at a time.

## 2. The single highest-value drug: one tablet, three targets

**Low-dose amitriptyline has good RCT evidence in both IBS and migraine prevention.** One drug covering
two of her three symptom domains — plus sleep — is worth more to her right now than three drugs covering
one each.

**IBS — Evidence A.** The **ATLANTIS trial** (*Lancet*, 2023) randomized **463 patients** across 55 UK
general practices. Amitriptyline was superior on the IBS Severity Scoring System (adjusted difference
**−27.0**), patients were roughly **twice as likely to report global improvement**, and it was safe and
well tolerated. This is the best-quality evidence for any oral drug in IBS.

**Migraine — Evidence B.** AAN/AHS Level B, "probably effective." The only antidepressant with
consistent migraine-prevention evidence.

**Dosing:** start **10 mg at bedtime**, patient-led titration to **30 mg** over 3 weeks (the ATLANTIS
protocol). Allow **6–8 weeks** at an effective dose before judging migraine benefit.

**Bonuses:** the anticholinergic effect helps diarrhea-predominant symptoms; the sedation helps sleep.
**Costs:** dry mouth, constipation, morning grogginess, weight gain.

> ### ⚠️ A safety catch worth flagging to whoever prescribes for her migraine
>
> **Propranolol and other beta-blockers are standard migraine preventives — but beta-blockade is
> associated with more severe and epinephrine-refractory anaphylaxis.**
>
> In a woman with an unexplained anaphylaxis history, **a TCA is the better dual-purpose choice than
> propranolol.** This is easy to miss because the migraine prescriber and the allergist are different
> people.

**Sequencing conflict:** amitriptyline is a 7–14 day skin-test suppressor. Decide deliberately — skin
testing first, or start now and hold for 14 days before the appointment. Either is fine; discovering
it in the waiting room is not.

## 3. The medication-overuse headache trap

This may be the whole headache story, and it is invisible unless someone counts.

**ICHD-3 thresholds:** simple analgesics (acetaminophen, NSAIDs, aspirin) **≥15 days/month for
>3 months**; triptans, ergots, opioids, combination analgesics **≥10 days/month**.

**Two traps that apply specifically to her:**

1. **All non-opioid analgesics count as one class.** Alternating ibuprofen and acetaminophen "to avoid
   overusing either" is exactly the behavior that produces medication-overuse headache.
2. **She may be taking analgesics for abdominal pain and joint pain, not headache — and it still
   causes it.** The receptor does not care about the indication. Someone on daily ibuprofen for joint
   pain plus acetaminophen for cramps can be in analgesic overuse without ever thinking of herself as
   treating a headache.

**This alone could explain why a decade of headaches never improved, including through the year of
meat avoidance.** Adding a preventive on top of ongoing overuse tends to fail — the overuse has to be
addressed too.

**Start counting acute medication days today.** NSAIDs also deserve scrutiny as a direct cause of her
abdominal pain and diarrhea.

## 4. The histamine question, handled properly

### Porcine DAO — do not take it

**The only commercially approved DAO supplement is porcine kidney protein extract.**

She has a **pork-specific IgE of 14.40 — her highest value — and has never eaten pork in her life.**
A porcine kidney supplement would be her **first pork protein exposure ever**, taken at home,
unsupervised, without an epinephrine protocol, in a woman who has already had anaphylaxis to
mammalian meat. Kidney is heavily perfused and a plausible vehicle for residual serum albumin — the
exact allergen family in question.

**Honest confidence: this is a mechanistic risk argument, not a documented harm.** No published case
report exists. But the efficacy evidence for DAO is near-zero — as of late 2024 no adequately
controlled trial had reported — and the theoretical downside is anaphylaxis. That risk-benefit is not
close.

Plant-derived DAO sidesteps the mammalian issue but has even thinner evidence. Also not recommended.

### Serum DAO testing is not valid

Do not order it, and do not act on one she has already had. In a 2023 evaluation, sensitivity at one
manufacturer cut-off was **2%**. A 2024 study found serum DAO **did not change** when patients moved
between a low-histamine and an ordinary diet — the number does not track the thing it claims to
measure. **A low DAO does not justify an indefinite diet.**

### A low-histamine trial, if done, needs a written stop rule

- **Prerequisite:** celiac bloods, component panel, and baseline tryptase already drawn.
- **Phase 1 — 14 days. Absolute maximum 21.** One variable only — no simultaneous FODMAP or gluten
  restriction.
- **⛔ The stop rule, written down on day 0: no clear diary-documented improvement by day 21 means the
  diet has failed. She resumes normal eating.** No extension, no "maybe I wasn't strict enough."
  **This sentence is what prevents a fourth permanent restriction being added to her life.**
- **Phase 2 — mandatory reintroduction, 3–4 weeks.** One category at a time, full serving, 3 days
  observation. Most responders tolerate far more than the internet lists claim.

### Quercetin belongs on the stop list, not the treatment list

Evidence D for efficacy. Documented adverse effects include **headache, nausea and vomiting,
paresthesia** — two of her three presenting complaints. It also inhibits cytochrome P450 enzymes,
which matters if she is about to start amitriptyline. Most products pair it with **bromelain**, a
pineapple protein — an added allergen exposure in a highly atopic patient.

## 5. Start today, interferes with nothing

**Gut-directed hypnotherapy has Evidence A and she can begin this week without a prescription,
referral, or appointment.**

- 2025 systematic review: **12 RCTs, 1,158 adults — all 12 favored it, 9 significantly.**
- Across 16 randomized studies, **40–81% improved**, with benefit documented **durable to seven
  years** — a profile no drug here can match.
- **Group and digital delivery are comparably effective** to individual face-to-face sessions.
- The **Nerva** app (Monash University) is a validated 6-week self-guided program.

**No prescription. No waiting list. No drug interactions. Cannot cause a reaction. Interferes with no
test.** For someone waiting months for specialists, that combination is close to unique.

**GI-focused CBT** is the companion, also Evidence A. In the trial behind the FDA clearance (n=558),
IBS severity fell **−133.3** with therapist-delivered CBT and **−101.2** with minimal-contact web
delivery, versus **−52.9** for usual care. **Mahana IBS** is FDA-cleared and prescribable by a PCP;
**Zemedy** is an automated alternative whose RCT specifically improved the **Fear of Food
Questionnaire** and **Visceral Sensitivity Index.**

Those two outcomes matter most for her. A decade has taught her that food causes catastrophe — a
lesson that is **correct** for beef, lamb and pork and **overgeneralized** to everything else.
GI-CBT targets the overgeneralization without ever asking her to doubt the real allergy.

## 6. GI symptom relief, ranked by evidence

**Soluble fiber — Evidence A.** ACG-recommended. Meta-analysis of 14 RCTs: **NNT 7**. **Insoluble
fiber (wheat bran) does not work and may worsen symptoms.** Start **3–5 g psyllium once daily**,
increasing every 1–2 weeks. Most "psyllium made me worse" stories are people starting at a full dose.
Choose plain husk — **no added inulin, sugar alcohols or sweeteners**, which are FODMAPs and will
confound everything.

**Enteric-coated peppermint oil — Evidence A/B.** Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs, 1,030 patients: **NNT 4**
for global symptoms, **NNT 7** for abdominal pain. Honest caveat: the NNT worsened from the previous
meta-analysis and two recent trials found no difference from placebo. **180–200 mg TID, 30–60 min
before meals**, 4 weeks. **Main side effect is heartburn** — avoid if she has reflux. Check the
capsule shell for gelatin.

**H1 antihistamines, up-dosed — Evidence B/C.** Second-generation base, with guideline-endorsed
up-dosing to 4× standard dose. Do it **stepwise and documented** — standard × 2 weeks, double × 2
weeks, 4× × 2 weeks — not "take more when it's bad." Fexofenadine is least sedating.

**H2 blocker — Evidence C.** Famotidine 20 mg twice daily. Cheap, safe, consensus-level rather than
RCT-proven.

**Oral cromolyn — Evidence C.** Not systemically absorbed; acts on gut mucosa only. **200 mg four
times daily, 30 min before meals and at bedtime.** Start at 100 mg once or twice daily and build over
2–4 weeks. **Do not judge before 4 weeks; full effect may take 8.** The real obstacle is adherence —
four doses daily, each premeal, from glass ampules, at meaningful cost. **If she cannot commit to 4
weeks of QID dosing, the trial produces a false negative and she wrongly crosses it off.**

**Ketotifen — Evidence B, with an access problem.** Best trial evidence in this class, but **oral
ketotifen is not FDA-approved in the US** — compounding pharmacy only, out of pocket. Reserve for
failure of high-dose H1.

**Low-FODMAP — Evidence A for efficacy, and the highest-risk intervention on this list for her.**
Every published trial was rated high risk of bias. Done properly it is three phases with restriction
limited to **2–6 weeks** and mandatory reintroduction; **up to 76% of responders successfully
liberalize.** Done badly — meaning stopped after phase 1, which is what most people do — it reduces
fiber, calcium and diet quality, and increases anxiety and disordered eating.

> **Guidance is now explicit that clinicians should screen for eating disorders before starting
> low-FODMAP.** Nearly **24% of patients referred to neurogastroenterology screen positive for probable
> ARFID.** A woman with a decade of GI symptoms, a year of mandated meat avoidance, bread avoidance and
> food fear is exactly the patient in whom an unsupervised low-FODMAP diet becomes a permanent identity.
> **Put this fourth or fifth, with a dietitian, after ARFID screening, with the reintroduction date on
> the calendar from day one.**

## 7. Nutrition — and the loops that sustain the illness

A decade of GI symptoms plus a year without her densest sources of heme iron, B12 and zinc makes
deficiency likely, not hypothetical. Several of these **cause the symptoms she is presenting with.**

| Deficiency | Loop it closes |
| --- | --- |
| **Iron** | Headache, fatigue, poor exercise tolerance — which then blocks the exercise intervention |
| **B12** (check **methylmalonic acid**, which catches what a "normal" B12 misses) | Nausea, anorexia, diarrhea, cognitive fog — GI *and* neuro symptoms from one cause |
| **Vitamin D** | **Diffuse musculoskeletal and joint pain** — a candidate explanation for the joint pain attributed to bread |
| **Magnesium** | Migraine and cramps. Serum magnesium is insensitive; normal does not exclude |
| **Zinc** | **Diarrhea** → zinc loss → more diarrhea |
| **Vitamin C** | **Scurvy causes arthralgia**, gum bleeding, bruising. Genuinely under-recognized in restrictive-diet patients |
| **Thiamine** | Nausea, anorexia, GI dysmotility |
| **Copper** (only if she takes zinc) | **Zinc supplementation induces copper deficiency** → cytopenias, myelopathy |

**Also draw: TSH + free T4** (hypothyroidism mimics her whole presentation and is fully reversible) and
**CMP with LFTs** (baseline before anything hepatically metabolized, and after a year of unknown herbal
products).

**Celiac disease sits at the centre of every loop on this list** — it causes the GI symptoms, the
malabsorption, the headache, the joint pain, *and* the deficiencies that independently cause more
headache and joint pain. It is also the cheapest thing to test for and the one destroyed by the
intervention she is most likely to reach for.

**Two repletion notes that matter:**
- **Iron: every-other-day dosing absorbs better and is better tolerated than daily** — daily dosing
  raises hepcidin and blocks the next dose, and halving the doses halves the GI side effects.
- **Magnesium glycinate, not oxide or citrate.** Oxide, citrate, sulfate and hydroxide are poorly
  absorbed and osmotically active — they cause **diarrhea indistinguishable from her disease.**
  Glycinate at **400–600 mg elemental** doubles as an AAN Level B migraine preventive.

## 8. The supplement washout — possibly the highest-yield free action available

### First: screen for mammalian-derived products. Urgent, not routine.

Given pork 14.40 and suspected albumin allergy, these are common in naturopathic practice and are
mammalian-derived:

**Desiccated porcine thyroid** (Armour, NP Thyroid) — pig thyroid tissue; **check for this first, and
do not stop it abruptly — it needs a physician to transition to synthetic levothyroxine** ·
**pancreatin / pancrelipase** (porcine pancreas) · **ox bile** · **bone broth protein powder**
(usually beef, a staple of gut protocols) · **colostrum, whey, beef organ capsules, glandulars,
"adrenal support"** · **collagen peptides, gelatin, gummy supplements** · **chondroitin** ·
**gelatin capsule shells on anything.**

### Supplements that plausibly cause her exact symptoms

| Supplement | What it causes |
| --- | --- |
| **Magnesium oxide/citrate** | Diarrhea, cramping |
| **Vitamin C >1–2 g** | Diarrhea, nausea, abdominal pain |
| **⚠️ Vitamin B6 / high-dose B-complex** | **Peripheral neuropathy.** Cases at <500 mg/day; Australia's TGA cut the permitted dose from 200 mg to 100 mg. **The RDA is 1.3 mg** |
| **⚠️ Niacin** | **Flushing, warmth, itching, headache — an excellent mimic of a histamine reaction.** Could be generating "histamine attacks" outright |
| **⚠️ Turmeric/curcumin**, especially with piperine | **Drug-induced liver injury** — now among the leading causes of herbal DILI in the US |
| **Quercetin (± bromelain)** | Headache, nausea, paresthesia; CYP interactions |
| **Inulin, FOS, chicory root, "prebiotic fiber"** | Bloating, distension, pain — these are FODMAPs, added to countless gut-health powders |
| **Sugar alcohols** in gummies | Osmotic diarrhea, bloating |
| **High-dose vitamin A / cod liver oil** | **Headache, nausea, joint and bone pain** |
| **High-dose vitamin D** | Hypercalcemia → nausea, abdominal pain, headache |
| **Berberine, oregano oil, wormwood** | Nausea, cramping, diarrhea |
| **Caffeine / "energy" products** | **Withdrawal headache** masquerading as her primary headache |
| **St John's wort** | Induces CYP3A4; **interacts with TCAs and triptans (serotonin syndrome)** |

### How to run it as an n-of-1 trial

1. **Photograph every label.** Sort into three piles: prescriptions (change nothing without the
   prescriber), withdrawal-risk items (caffeine — taper over 1–2 weeks or the withdrawal headache
   ruins the trial), and everything else.
2. **Run the diary for 7 days first** to establish a baseline.
3. **Stop the entire "everything else" pile on a single day.** Not serially — the goal is a clean
   baseline she has not had in a year, not culprit identification. Serial stopping takes six months
   and answers nothing.
4. **Observe 4 weeks, change nothing else.** Expect possible worsening in week 1 from caffeine taper.
   Judge on weeks 3–4.
5. **Better?** They were part of the problem. **Unchanged?** Excellent information — the field is now
   clean. **Worse?** Reintroduce one item per week, only those with a genuine indication.

**The default end state: nothing except what a measured deficiency or a guideline indication
justifies.**

## 10. Lifestyle — what actually has evidence

**Sleep — highest yield for headache.** Sleep disruption drives migraine chronification, and acute
sleep deprivation lowers the threshold for cortical spreading depolarization — a real mechanism, not a
platitude. **Regularity matters more than duration: a fixed wake time seven days a week**, including
weekends, is the single most effective intervention. Bedtime amitriptyline gives a third target for
the same tablet.

**Exercise — Evidence A/B for both targets.** In migraine RCTs, structured aerobic exercise reduced
attack frequency by **26–63%**. In IBS, a structured walking program reduced symptom severity by
**43.8%**. **Start with walking, 20–30 min most days** — the modality with IBS-specific trial data.
**Check iron status first**; trying to exercise while iron-deficient is how these programs fail.
One caution: no vigorous exercise within 4 hours of an unfamiliar meal, given her anaphylaxis history.

**Alcohol — more nuanced than the internet claims.** In migraine, red wine produced an attack in only
**8.8%** of participants on formal challenge, and a meta-analysis found wine **not associated** with
migraine. In IBS, **binge drinking (≥4 drinks/day) predicted next-day symptoms; light-to-moderate
drinking did not.** **The pattern matters more than the total.** She does not need another total
prohibition — she has enough of those, and a needless one costs her social life without buying health.
(Exception: alcohol must be excluded during a phase-1 low-histamine trial.)

## 11. Psychological support, framed correctly

**How not to frame it:** she has spent ten years being disbelieved, was given a label that turned out
to be wrong, eliminated meat for a year on the strength of it and got no better, then spent another
year and considerable money on treatment that also failed. If this arrives as "we can't find anything,
let's talk about stress," she will reject it — **and she will be right to**, because her pork IgE of
14.40 and her anaphylaxis are organic, documented and dangerous.

**How to frame it — two things are true at once:**

1. She has a **real, potentially life-threatening IgE-mediated food allergy** that has been serially
   mismanaged. The workup is the priority.
2. **A decade of pain, fear and disbelief produces measurable changes in visceral pain processing.**
   Central sensitization is physiological, not imaginary, and there are treatments with RCT evidence
   that target it. Declining them does not make the allergy more real; it leaves her in more pain.

### Screen for ARFID before prescribing any further restriction

ARFID's recognized drivers include **"avoidance of food due to fear of aversive consequences"** —
precisely what a decade of unpredictable reactions plus genuine anaphylaxis manufactures. Food-allergic
patients are documented to develop avoidance extending well beyond the medically necessary diet.

**The tool: the Nine Item ARFID Screen (NIAS).** Two minutes, validated in adults, with a subscale
specifically for fear of aversive consequences.

**How to raise it without insult:** frame it as protecting her nutrition and quality of life from a
decade of restriction — not as questioning whether the allergy is real. **The critical distinction is
between the medically required avoidance (beef, lamb, pork — permanent, correct, non-negotiable) and
the accrued avoidance (everything else she has stopped eating without a test that justified it).**
ARFID treatment for a food-allergic patient does not touch the first category.

### The sentence worth saying to her

> *"Your alpha-gal test was negative and your pork IgE is 14.40. You were given the wrong label. The
> reactions were always real; the explanation was wrong. Here is the right one, and here is what we do
> about it."*

## Where this is weak

The mast-cell and histamine interventions rest on small trials, case series and expert consensus.
**The porcine DAO warning is mechanistic, not documented** — no published case of DAO-supplement
anaphylaxis in a pork-allergic patient exists. It is a recommendation against because the efficacy
evidence is near-zero and the theoretical downside is anaphylaxis, not because harm has been proven.

**The pork-cat hypothesis remains unconfirmed.** It fits well, but the component panel is what settles
it. **Nothing in this plan depends on it being right.**

Skin-test washout periods vary between centers. **Hers sets the rule** — confirm with her testing
center rather than this table.
