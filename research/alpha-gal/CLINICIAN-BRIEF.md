# Consultation Brief

**A one-page summary to hand to an allergist-immunologist at the start of the appointment.**

Written as a clinical question, not a patient self-diagnosis. The aim is to get component testing
ordered. Print it, hand it over, and let it do the talking.

---

## Why this framing

The single biggest risk to this appointment is that it reads as a patient who has been reading online
and arrived with a diagnosis. That gets dismissed, and dismissal costs another year.

What does *not* get dismissed is a **specific, internally consistent laboratory discordance** with a
**named, testable hypothesis** and a **request for two specific component tests.** Allergists find
discordant serology genuinely interesting. Lead with the numbers.

**Do not open with "I think I have pork-cat syndrome."**
**Open with "My alpha-gal is undetectable but my pork IgE is 14.4 and I've never eaten pork."**

The second sentence makes an allergist sit forward. The first one makes them sit back.

---

## The brief

> ### Consultation request — discordant meat-specific IgE
>
> **Presenting question:** I carry a diagnosis of alpha-gal syndrome made approximately one year ago.
> My alpha-gal IgE has been undetectable on both occasions it has been measured. I would like help
> understanding my serology and determining whether the diagnosis is correct.
>
> ### Laboratory results
>
> | Test | Result |
> | --- | --- |
> | Galactose-α-1,3-galactose (o215) IgE | **`<0.10 kU/L`** — at diagnosis and one year later |
> | Pork IgE | **14.40 kU/L** |
> | Lamb IgE | **10.20 kU/L** |
> | Beef IgE | **2.38 kU/L** |
> | Total serum IgE | **Not obtained** |
> | Baseline serum tryptase | **Not obtained** |
>
> ### The discordance
>
> **I have never eaten pork**, yet pork is my highest specific IgE.
>
> ### Clinical history
>
> - **Reactions are immediate**, beginning within minutes of eating
> - **Sequence:** oropharyngeal pruritus → dizziness → nausea → headache
> - **Trigger foods:** steak and lamb. Steak frequently cooked in butter, but reactions have occurred
>   without it
> - I also report reactions to dairy and to some medications
> - **Cat ownership for most of my life**, ending approximately 3–4 years ago
> - Separately: approximately 10 years of GI-predominant symptoms (abdominal pain, nausea, diarrhea)
>   plus headache, which did **not** improve during a year of complete mammalian meat avoidance
>
> ### What I would like to ask
>
> 1. Given an undetectable alpha-gal IgE with elevated meat-specific IgE, is alpha-gal syndrome still
>    the correct diagnosis, or is my meat-specific IgE directed at protein rather than carbohydrate
>    epitopes?
> 2. Given lifelong cat exposure and high IgE to a meat I have never eaten, **would you consider
>    mammalian serum albumin sensitization** — and would **Fel d 2 (e220)** and **Bos d 6 (e204)**
>    testing be reasonable?
> 3. Could I have a **total serum IgE** so my specific IgE values can be interpreted in proportion?
> 4. Could I have a **baseline serum tryptase**? I have significant histamine-related symptoms that
>    have never been objectively assessed.
> 5. My reactions have involved three organ systems including presyncope. **Should I be carrying an
>    epinephrine auto-injector?** I do not currently have one.
>
> ### One ordering note
>
> Combined cat allergen panels typically reflex to components only if whole-cat IgE is ≥0.35 kU/L. I
> have been without cats for 3–4 years and that value may have declined, which would prevent the
> component from running. **Could Fel d 2 be ordered as a standalone component rather than through a
> reflex panel?**

---

## How to handle the likely responses

**"Your alpha-gal was probably a false negative — seronegative alpha-gal syndrome exists."**
It does, at roughly 2% of referred patients. But those patients are typically **negative or low across
the meat panel as well**, because in alpha-gal syndrome the meat IgE *is* alpha-gal IgE measured
through a different reagent. A pork value of 14.40 with an undetectable alpha-gal is the opposite
pattern. Reasonable reply: *"Would the meat-specific IgE also be low if the alpha-gal assay were
missing it?"*

**"The meat panel results are probably clinically irrelevant sensitization."**
That may partly be true, and it is worth taking seriously — sensitization is far more common than
allergy. But it does not explain a decade of reproducible, stereotyped, multi-system reactions to two
specific foods. Reasonable reply: *"That's possible — would a total IgE help put these in
proportion?"*

**"Component testing isn't necessary; just avoid mammalian meat."**
Fair as management, but it misses two things worth naming: the mechanism determines **whether cooking
matters**, which affects how restricted her diet needs to be; and it determines **which non-food
exposures are dangerous** — bovine serum albumin surgical adhesives are a documented cause of
intraoperative anaphylaxis in albumin-mediated allergy and would not be flagged under an alpha-gal
label.

**"Where did you get this idea?"**
Answer honestly: *"I had research done on my lab results and the pattern was flagged. I'm not
attached to the conclusion — I'd like your read on whether the discordance means anything."* Inviting
their judgment is what turns this from a confrontation into a consultation.

---

## Getting to the right clinician

**Any academic allergy-immunology department** can order component-resolved diagnostics. This does not
require a rare-disease specialist — it requires someone willing to look at the discordance.

**Screening question when booking:** *"Does the allergist order component-resolved testing —
individual allergen components like Fel d 2 or Bos d 6 — rather than only whole-extract panels?"* A
practice that routinely does component testing will answer yes immediately.

**Bring to the appointment:** the original lab reports (not summaries), a written reaction timeline
with latencies, a complete medication and supplement list including capsule types, and this brief.

---

## Two things not to let get lost

**1. The epinephrine question.** Reactions involving three organ systems including presyncope meet
anaphylaxis criteria. This should not leave the appointment unresolved.

**2. The chronic illness is a separate problem.** Ten years of GI symptoms did not respond to meat
avoidance. Whatever the meat allergy turns out to be, it does not explain that — and the appointment
should not end with the chronic symptoms folded into the allergy diagnosis. That conflation is what
produced the original error.

**Ask separately for celiac serology — tTG-IgA with total serum IgA — while still eating gluten.**
The antibodies decay within weeks of starting a gluten-free diet, so this test has a deadline that
none of the others do.
