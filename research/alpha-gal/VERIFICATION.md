# Verification Audit

**Read this before quoting any number from the dossier to a clinician.**

This research was conducted under two constraints that matter: an egress proxy blocked direct access
to essentially every journal host (PubMed, PMC, Wiley, ScienceDirect, JACI, ClinicalTrials.gov), and
the session's web-search budget was exhausted at 200 queries. **Most figures therefore come from
search-engine summaries of abstracts rather than from full text.**

That is good enough to build a strategy on. It is not good enough to make a clinical decision on
without confirmation. This document says exactly which claims carry weight, how confident each one is,
and what to check first.

---

## Tier 2 — Load-bearing but single-source. **Verify these first.**

Each of these does real work in the dossier and rests on one search result. **These are the priority
verification list.**

### ⚠️ 1. The reintroduction study — numbers conflict across sources

This is the most important discrepancy in the whole document, and it needs resolving.

Two different figure sets appeared:

- **263 patients → 21 (8%) eligible → 19 proceeded → 19/19 succeeded**
- **201 patients → 14 (7%) eligible → 12 elected to reintroduce**

**Most likely explanation:** these are two publications from the same group — an earlier conference
abstract (*JACI* 2024, S0091-6749(24)02076-1) and the full paper (*JACI: In Practice* 2025,
S2213-2198(25)01132-8), with the cohort having grown between them. The eligibility criteria and the
protocol are consistent across both.

**What is solid regardless:** eligibility was **alpha-gal IgE <0.35 kU/L plus no reactions on
avoidance**; the protocol was a **9-day graded home reintroduction** increasing quantity and fat
content; and **everyone who attempted it succeeded**. The strategy does not change either way.

**Verify:** the exact cohort size and success count, from the *JACI: In Practice* full text.

### 2. The mast cell hyperactivity cohort — the basis of the key clinical recommendation

Single source (*JACI* 2025, S0091-6749(25)01390-9). Figures used: **260 patients, 34.3% with MCH;
dairy tolerance 34.4% vs 69.1%; gelatin tolerance 13.8% vs 42.2%; migraine 21% vs 4%; MCH group median
IgE 1.9 vs 3.8 kU/L.**

**Why it matters:** this is the single most actionable finding in the dossier — the argument that her
symptom pattern reflects a described phenotype warranting mast-cell-directed treatment rather than
stricter avoidance.

**Verify:** all five figures, and the authors' definition of MCH. Note that even if the exact
percentages shift, the *direction* — that a substantial AGS subgroup has dairy/gelatin intolerance
plus migraine and responds incompletely to diet — is what the recommendation rests on.

### 3. The alpha-gal oral immunotherapy cases — the basis of the "second door"

Three separate single-source items:
- **Ünal et al., *Allergy* 2023** — 12 patients, all became tolerant, 3 terminated; 15-day and 27-day
  protocols; maintenance ≥100 g daily for 6 months
- **Telemedicine-monitored OIT** (*Ann Allergy* 2025, PMID 39818413) — **baseline IgE 10.9 kU/L**,
  prior severe anaphylaxis, updosed over ~4 months, **passed a 150 g steak challenge**
- **Modified 24-step desensitization**, pediatric, **IgE 5.2 kU/L**

**Why it matters:** these are the entire basis for the claim that she may not need to wait for
seroreversion. **This is the most consequential thing to verify**, because it could change her
timeline from years to months — and because recommending an experimental route on shaky sourcing would
be the most harmful error in this document.

**Verify:** all three, especially the telemedicine case's baseline IgE and outcome.

### 4. The heparin cardiac surgery data

Single source (*Ann Thorac Surg* 2021). Figures: **8,819 patients; 17 (0.19%) alpha-gal positive;
4/17 (24%) reacted, rising to 50% with recent IgE; median titer 75 IU/mL in reactors vs 8 in
non-reactors.**

**Why it matters:** it drives the perioperative safety card. The qualitative recommendation (screen
and premedicate before high-dose IV heparin) is echoed in multiple perioperative reviews, so the
guidance is safe even if the exact figures move.

### 5. Thyroglobulin as the most potent alpha-gal allergen

Single source (*Allergy* 2026, Mangaard et al.), reporting **EC50 below 10 pg/mL in all patients
tested**, ahead of pancreatic enzymes and pork kidney.

**Why it matters:** it is the reason desiccated thyroid sits at the top of the Tier 1 medication audit.
Note that porcine thyroid being mammalian-derived is not in dispute — the *potency ranking* is what
needs verifying, and the recommendation to prefer synthetic levothyroxine holds either way.

### 6. Permethrin field efficacy

**65% bite reduction year one, 50% year two.** Single source, and the qualitative recommendation is
uncontroversial.

## Tier 3 — Explicitly flagged as reasoning, not findings

These are clearly marked in the dossier, and are repeated here so nobody mistakes them for citations.

### The two-reservoir explanation for why no cure exists

**This is my synthesis, not a claim any single paper makes.** The components:

- **Verified with URLs:** two 2025 *Immunity* papers describing long-lived IgE plasma cells with a
  half-life exceeding 49 days, residing outside the bone marrow. **This is mouse work.** Whether
  humans have an equivalent splenic compartment is not established.
- **Well-established but not re-verified here:** the sequential class-switch model (IgE memory residing
  in IgG1⁺ memory B cells). The human evidence — single-cell repertoire studies showing IgE clones
  sharing lineage with IgG1 — is the part most worth checking, since the whole model rests on it.
  There is also a known dispute about one of the foundational reporter-mouse studies.
- **My reasoning:** that the opposite CD19/CD38 marker profiles of the two reservoirs explain why no
  approved agent cures allergy.

The arithmetic argument — that a 16-month half-life cannot reflect antibody clearance and must reflect
cellular attrition — is sound and independent of the citations.

### Omalizumab interfering with IgE monitoring

**Mechanistic inference, not a cited finding.** That omalizumab forms drug–IgE complexes and that
measured total IgE rises on therapy is well established. That *specific* IgE measurements become
uninterpretable for certifying the reintroduction threshold follows logically but was not verified
against a study. **Presented in the dossier as a question to ask the allergist, which is the right
framing.**

### Omalizumab-facilitated alpha-gal desensitization

**Explicitly labeled a hypothesis.** The three components are individually solid. The combination has
not been studied in alpha-gal syndrome, and the dossier says so plainly in every place it appears.

### The CHO-cell alpha-gal risk with omalizumab

Plausible and mechanistically sound (same pathway as cetuximab), and reportedly flagged in the
published AGS literature — but not independently verified. **The precaution costs nothing**, so acting
on it is reasonable regardless.

## One framing caveat worth carrying

A research agent raised a fair objection: **0.35 kU/L is a generic assay reporting cutoff, not a
validated alpha-gal reintroduction threshold.** Some patients tolerate meat well above it; others
react below it.

The dossier already handles this correctly — it presents 0.35 as the *eligibility gate the successful
study used* (empirically sufficient), and then devotes a whole section to desensitization succeeding at
5.2 and 10.9 kU/L (demonstrating it is not necessary). But if any single sentence gets quoted in
isolation, it should not be one implying 0.35 is a biological cure line. **It is a conservative
threshold that made a study safe, not a law of nature.**

## What to do with this

**For the allergist appointment:** the Tier 1 claims are safe to discuss as established. For Tier 2,
say "I read that…" and let them verify — a good specialist will know most of this literature or can
pull it in minutes.

**If anyone wants to check the primary sources:** the highest-value four, in order, are the
*JACI: In Practice* 2025 reintroduction paper, the *JACI* 2025 mast cell hyperactivity cohort, the
*Ann Allergy* 2025 telemedicine OIT case, and the *Allergy* 2023 Ünal OIT series. Those four carry
most of the document's practical weight. All are behind the proxy block that constrained this
research, but any hospital library or a physician's institutional access reaches them immediately.

**Nothing in the practical toolkit depends on contested figures.** The medication audit, the safety
card, and the symptom diary are built on qualitative facts (porcine and bovine products contain
alpha-gal; reactions are delayed; epinephrine comes first) that are not in dispute.
