Four working documents, rebuilt around the corrected diagnosis. Print or copy them — they are meant
to be used, not read.

# 1. Emergency and Procedure Card

**Give a copy to every treating clinician. Keep one in her wallet and photograph it on her phone.**

> ## ⚕️ ANAPHYLAXIS RISK — MAMMALIAN MEAT ALLERGY
>
> **Patient has an IgE-mediated allergy to mammalian meat.** Documented specific IgE:
> **pork 14.40, lamb 10.20, beef 2.38 kU/L.**
>
> **Alpha-gal IgE is `<0.10` — NEGATIVE.** She has previously been labeled "alpha-gal syndrome."
> **That label is not supported by her serology.** Her reactions are **IMMEDIATE**, not delayed.
>
> **Suspected mechanism: mammalian serum albumin (pork-cat syndrome), cross-sensitized from cat
> exposure. Pending confirmation by Fel d 2 / Bos d 6 component testing.**
>
> ### Reaction pattern
>
> Onset **within minutes**. Sequence: **itchy mouth and throat → dizziness → nausea → headache.**
> Prior reactions have involved **three organ systems** and meet criteria for **anaphylaxis**.
>
> ⚠️ **Do not expect a delayed 3–6 hour reaction.** Her reactions begin immediately, at the mouth.
>
> ### Emergency management
>
> **EPINEPHRINE FIRST — IM, anterolateral thigh. Then call 911.**
> Antihistamines do not treat anaphylaxis. Lie flat, legs elevated — do not sit or stand her up.
> Observe minimum **4 hours**, extending to 6+ with risk factors. Counsel on **biphasic reactions.**
>
> ### Avoid — foods
>
> All mammalian meat: **beef, lamb, pork, veal, goat, venison, bison, rabbit, and all organ meats.**
> **Include pork** — highest IgE, never clinically tested.
> Highest risk: **rare or medium meat**, and **cured, dried or smoked products** (prosciutto, salami,
> jerky), which are *more* reactive than well-cooked meat.
>
> ### Avoid — medical products
>
> | Product | Why |
> | --- | --- |
> | **Bovine serum albumin tissue adhesives / sealants** | Documented intraoperative anaphylaxis in this syndrome |
> | **Gelatin colloids** (Gelofusine, Gelaspan) | Mammalian-derived, IV route |
> | **Gelatin hemostatics** (Gelfoam, Surgifoam, Surgiflo, FloSeal) | Mammalian collagen |
> | **Heparin** (porcine/bovine) | Mammalian-derived |
> | **Bovine/porcine bioprosthetic valves, patches, xenograft** | Mammalian tissue |
> | **Cetuximab** | Contraindicated in mammalian meat allergy |
> | **Cell-culture products containing BSA** | Direct albumin exposure |
>
> **Substitutes:** oxidized regenerated cellulose (Surgicel), plant polysaccharide hemostats,
> **human recombinant** thrombin, synthetic sealants, crystalloid or albumin-free colloid,
> argatroban or bivalirudin in place of heparin.
>
> ### Cofactors that lower her threshold
>
> **Alcohol · exercise · NSAIDs · intercurrent illness.** No mammalian-derived food within ~4 hours
> of exercise, and no alcohol or NSAIDs around such meals.
>
> **Allergist: ________________  Phone: ________________  Blood type: ________**

# 2. Medication and Supplement Audit

**Why:** she reacts to multiple medications, and a year of naturopathic supplements has never been
inventoried. Mammalian-derived ingredients are common and manufacturers are not required to disclose
animal origin.

## Check for these

| Ingredient | Source | Found in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gelatin capsule shells** | Bovine/porcine | Most soft and hard capsules. Substitute: HPMC (plant) |
| **Bovine serum albumin** | Bovine blood | Some cell-culture-derived products, sealants |
| **Bovine colostrum** | Bovine | "Gut healing" protocols — double hit given dairy reactivity |
| **Collagen peptides** | Bovine/porcine | Ubiquitous. Marine collagen is the alternative |
| **Desiccated glandulars** (adrenal, thymus, liver) | Bovine/porcine | Naturopathic "adrenal support" |
| **Pancreatin, pepsin, ox bile** | Porcine/bovine | "Digestive enzyme complex" |
| **Desiccated thyroid** (Armour, NP Thyroid) | Porcine thyroid | If prescribed for hypothyroid symptoms |
| **Conjugated equine estrogens** (Premarin) | Mare urine | Hormone therapy |
| **Serum-derived bovine immunoglobulin** | Bovine plasma | Marketed for "leaky gut" |
| **Bone broth protein powders** | Bovine | Gut protocols |

## Supplements that may be *causing* symptoms

A year of supplements without improvement makes this worth ruling out. Common culprits for nausea,
abdominal pain, diarrhea or headache: **magnesium** (osmotic diarrhea) · **vitamin C >1 g**
(osmotic diarrhea) · **zinc** on an empty stomach (nausea) · **sugar alcohols** in flavored powders
(bloating, diarrhea) · **inulin/FOS** in probiotics (gas, pain) · **berberine, oregano oil**
(nausea, cramping) · **quercetin ≥1 g** (headache, nausea) · **non-DGL licorice** (hypertension,
hypokalemia, headache) · **high-dose vitamin A or D** (headache, nausea).

**Free n-of-1 trial:** photograph every bottle, stop everything non-essential for 4 weeks, track
symptoms daily, then reintroduce one at a time. Run this in parallel with testing.

## Tests not worth ordering

**Serum DAO** (not a validated diagnostic) · **IgG/IgG4 food sensitivity panels** (measure exposure
and tolerance, not disease — a primary engine of unnecessary restriction) · **hair mineral analysis**
· **urine mycotoxin testing** · **untargeted broad food IgE panels** (guarantee more clinically
irrelevant positives in a patient who already carries one wrong label).

# 3. Symptom and Exposure Diary

**Two timestamps are the whole point.** Her reactions are immediate — that is a key diagnostic
finding and it should be documented rather than remembered.

```
DATE: __________

── INTAKE ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Time      Food / medication / supplement (be specific)
_____     ___________________________________________________________
_____     ___________________________________________________________

For any meat:  [ ] rare  [ ] medium  [ ] well done  [ ] slow-cooked/braised
               [ ] cured/smoked/dried        Cooked in butter?  [ ] yes  [ ] no

── COFACTORS (circle) ────────────────────────────────────────────────
  alcohol   exercise   NSAIDs   illness   poor sleep   stress   menstruation

── REACTION ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Onset time: _____     →  LATENCY (onset − intake): _____ min / hours

First symptom: ______________________________________________________
Then (circle):  itchy mouth   itchy throat   throat tightness   hives
                swelling   dizziness   faintness   nausea   vomiting
                abdominal pain   diarrhea   headache/migraine   wheeze

Severity:  [ ]1 mild  [ ]2  [ ]3 moderate  [ ]4  [ ]5 severe
Epinephrine used?  [ ] yes  [ ] no      Treatment: ______________________
```

### What to look for after four weeks

- **Latency.** Consistently under an hour supports the albumin mechanism and argues against alpha-gal.
- **Cooking degree.** Reactions to rare but not to slow-cooked meat would be strong evidence for a
  heat-labile allergen. **Record it; do not test it deliberately.**
- **Cofactor clustering.** Reactions on alcohol/exercise/NSAID days is the most modifiable finding here.
- **Symptom days with no mammalian intake at all.** A high count points to the separate, undiagnosed
  illness rather than the food allergy — which is the single most useful thing this diary can show.

## If only one thing gets done

**Celiac serology — tTG-IgA with total serum IgA — drawn before any gluten-free trial.** It is the
only item with a deadline: the antibodies decay within weeks of stopping gluten, and a missed
diagnosis here would explain a decade of symptoms that meat avoidance never touched.
