## ⚡ The two things that matter, before anything else

Everything else in this document is discussion. **These two are actions, they are cheap, and neither
depends on resolving a single item in the differential.**

> #### ⚠️ And there is a specific, foreseeable route to a severe reaction that nobody has named
>
> She was told that **three rounds of SAAT (soft-laser "allergy antidote" acupuncture) cured her
> gluten allergy.** If she — or that practitioner — draws the obvious next inference, that SAAT can
> also clear her **meat** allergy, **she will deliberately eat steak to prove it.** With no
> epinephrine in the house.
>
> That is not a hypothetical. It is the predictable consequence of the belief she has already been

### 2. tTG-IgA + total serum IgA — drawn before she stops eating bread

⏳ **This is the only genuinely unrecoverable window in the entire case.**

She is still eating gluten. The moment she starts a gluten-free trial — which her own bread belief
makes very likely, and which *reading this document will accelerate* — tTG-IgA falls and duodenal
histology normalizes over weeks. She then becomes undiagnosable without a formal 6–8 week gluten
challenge that she will not want to do, and may spend the rest of her life gluten-free without ever
knowing whether she needed to be, and without the care a real celiac diagnosis triggers: bone
density, nutrient monitoring, first-degree-relative screening, associated autoimmune surveillance.

**Total IgA is mandatory, not optional.** Selective IgA deficiency is ~1:500 in the general
population and 10–15× enriched in celiac; a lone tTG-IgA in an IgA-deficient patient is a false
negative. If IgA-deficient → DGP-IgG or tTG-IgG.

**Do not lead with HLA-DQ2/DQ8.** Negative predictive value is ~99%, but **30–40% of the general
population carries DQ2/DQ8**, so a positive tells you nothing. It is the fallback for someone
already off gluten, not a first-line test.

> ### 🛑 The single most important instruction in this document
>
> **Do not change her diet until the celiac result returns.** Not gluten, not anything. Two weeks of
> patience protects a diagnosis that lasts a lifetime.

### ⚠️ Re-correction: I over-adjusted, and the reason is that my prediction was wrong to begin with

I called doneness "close to decisive." **It never was**, because the textbook claim that serum
albumin is thermolabile is itself overstated:

- **Restani et al. 1998** — the decisive datum. *Heat treatment and chemical denaturation were **not**
  able to decrease BSA's capacity to bind circulating IgE.* Albumin's antigenicity is only partially
  structural; much of the IgE response is to **sequential epitopes**, and sequential epitopes do not
  care about cooking.
- **Fiocchi et al. 1998** — of 11 beef-allergic children, **7 were skin-test positive to heated BSA
  and 4 had a positive double-blind challenge to it.** Only industrially sterilized beef was
  uniformly safe. **Domestic cooking is not enough.**
- **Posthumus et al. 2013**, the original US pork-cat series, describes reactions to **smoked ham and
  sausage** — fully cooked, cured products.
- There is a published case report titled *"Pork-cat syndrome as cause of anaphylactic reaction to
  well-cooked meat."*
- Porcine albumin retained **52%** of its binding after 100 °C for 20 minutes and **29%** after
  autoclaving at 121 °C — far more aggressive than any kitchen.

**So a mixed-doneness history is roughly what an albumin mechanism predicts anyway.** Working the
likelihood ratio: P(mixed | albumin) ≈ 0.55, P(mixed | heat-stable allergen) ≈ 0.90, LR ≈ 0.61 —
attenuated toward 1 by unverifiable restaurant doneness, decade-old recall, and cofactor variability.
**Posterior ≈ 45–50%. Call it 48%.** The answer costs the hypothesis about ten points, not thirty.

**It is a nudge, not a refutation, and the right response is to test rather than re-rank.**

What still stands from my first reading: the correction in `CASE-RECONSTRUCTION.md` §3 was right —
"just cook it well done" was never a safe plan, and now there is history suggesting it does not work
for her. And Fel d 2 remains the fulcrum.

## 0c. The postprandial pattern — a search heuristic, not a syndrome

Her joint pain **happens after food.** Not inflammatory (no morning stiffness), not mechanical (not
use-related). **Meal-linked.**

### ⚠️ Correction to my first reading of this

I initially wrote that she may have "one postprandial multi-system symptom complex" and called a
post-meal tryptase "the best idea in this document." Research review disciplined both claims:

**There is no recognized syndrome called postprandial multi-system disease.** Postprandial *GI*
syndromes are well defined. Postprandial *cardiovascular* syndromes are well defined. **Postprandial
arthralgia is essentially absent from the primary literature as an entity.** What exists instead are
conditions that are *themselves* multi-system and happen to worsen after meals — celiac, clonal or
hereditary mast cell disease, dysautonomia.

**The honest reframe:** if her symptoms really are meal-locked, that is a **clue** pointing at a small
number of individually testable diagnoses — plus a much larger probability mass of overlapping
disorders of gut–brain interaction. It is a search heuristic. It is not a diagnosis, and I should not
have implied otherwise.

### ⭐ And before any of it: the free confound check nobody has run

> **"Postprandial" and "later in the day" are confounded, and nobody has separated them for her.**
>
> Meals cluster in the afternoon and evening. So does cumulative joint use, fatigue, and circadian
> symptom drift. **A patient who says "it happens after food" on direct questioning may be reporting
> "it happens in the afternoon."**

**The single most valuable thing she can produce before her next appointment** is not a test. It is a
**time-stamped log with onset latency** — not "after food" but *"meal 13:10, joint pain 15:40."*

Then plot two things:

1. **A latency histogram** for each symptom — how many minutes after a meal it starts.
2. **The confound plot:** does joint pain track *time since last meal*, or *time of day*? If it is
   flat against meal latency but rises through the afternoon regardless of when she ate, **the
   postprandial hypothesis is dead and months have been saved for free.**

### ⚠️ The latency also decides whether the tryptase idea is worth doing at all

Tryptase rises within **30–60 minutes** and decays with a **~2 hour half-life**.

- **Joint pain starting 4–12 hours after eating** → mast cell mediator release is not a plausible
  mechanism, and no amount of post-meal tryptase drawing will show anything.
- **Symptoms within 1–2 hours** → the measurement becomes worth attempting.

**And even then, expectations need lowering.** In Dua et al.'s 160 adult peanut challenges —
unambiguous, objectively observed, IgE-mediated food reactions — a tryptase rise meeting a 30%
threshold had **53% sensitivity** (specificity 0.85, AUC 0.72). Median rise across all reactions was
25%; it reached 71% only during frank anaphylaxis. **A negative post-meal tryptase would therefore
prove very little.** I presented this as a clean yes-or-no answer. It is not one.

There is also **no published protocol** for post-meal tryptase in suspected food-triggered mast cell
disease. What can be offered is the oral-food-challenge design repurposed — present it to an
allergist as an adaptation, not a standard.

⚠️ **One more constraint:** under the ECNM/AIM consensus criteria, only respiratory, cardiovascular,
gastrointestinal, naso-ocular and cutaneous systems count toward the "≥2 organ systems" requirement
for MCAS. **Musculoskeletal symptoms do not count.** Her joint pain cannot be used as evidence *for*
a mast cell diagnosis, even though patients with proven mast cell disease report it.

**What still stands:** the **baseline** tryptase. Cheap, no timing required, does two jobs — anchors
any future calculation and screens for hereditary alpha-tryptasemia. That she has carried a mast cell
label for ten years without one is the clearest single gap in this case.

### 🩺 The measurement I underweighted, and it is the cheapest objective thing available

**Postprandial hypotension.** A fall in systolic BP of **≥20 mmHg within 2 hours of a meal.** She has
dizziness — this would be an **objectively measured, multi-system postprandial finding**, and it
explains dizziness directly and plausibly contributes to headache. *(It explains nothing at all about
joint pain, which is worth saying.)*

**Protocol, at home, with a cuff:** seated and rested 5 minutes · BP + HR immediately pre-meal · then
**every 15 minutes for 2 hours**, recording symptoms at each point · repeat on ≥3 days, including one
high-carbohydrate meal. *(The published evidence supports every 10 minutes for 60 minutes; every 15
is a practical compromise and she should say so.)*

Add an **active stand test** once: supine 10 minutes, then HR and BP at 1, 3, 5 and 10 minutes
standing. POTS is a sustained HR rise ≥30 bpm within 10 minutes without orthostatic hypotension.

Prevalence is not a geriatric curiosity: **~25% in normotensive young adults**, and **23.2% of adults
presenting with postprandial symptoms** who underwent a mixed-meal test. Most POTS patients report
worsening specifically after **carbohydrate-rich** meals — a proposed mechanism being greater GIP
release causing splanchnic vasodilation.

### ⭐ And the one history question that may resolve most of the food-blaming

> **"Does the urge to open your bowels come within 15–30 minutes of starting a meal, regardless of
> what the meal was?"**
>
> If yes, **that is the gastrocolic response** — an exaggerated but normal reflex, prominent in IBS-D,
> and **not a food-specific reaction.** IBS-D patients show significantly greater postprandial sigmoid
> motor activity and faster postprandial transit. This single question could dissolve a large part of
> a decade of food attribution.

### The evidence against acute meal-triggered joint pain, stated plainly

Because she deserves the counter-evidence, not just the hypothesis:

- **The PIRA trial** — randomized controlled crossover, 25 patients, isocaloric red meat vs salmon vs
  soy meals, blood sampled to 5 hours — found **no significant difference in postprandial IL-6 or
  triglycerides between meals.** It was designed against exactly the belief that red meat aggravates
  inflammatory joint disease.
- **Metabolic endotoxemia** — the "meal → gut permeability → LPS → systemic inflammation → joints"
  story — is not established. A 2025 critical review states it **remains unclear whether a meaningful
  amount of bioactive gut-derived LPS reaches the bloodstream at all.**
- **Celiac arthralgia is real** — 10.7% of celiac patients without rheumatic comorbidity have joint
  complaints — but it is a **chronic immune-mediated phenomenon, not an acute per-meal event.** Celiac
  would explain "joint pain in a person who eats gluten." It would not neatly explain "joint pain 90
  minutes after this meal."
- **The migraine parallel is the sharpest warning.** Patient-reported migraine food triggers
  repeatedly fail blinded challenge, and some "triggers" — food craving, meal-skipping — are **early
  manifestations of the premonitory phase of the attack itself.** The attack causes the eating, not
  the reverse. She has chronic headache. This applies directly.

**The three most probable explanations for her postprandial joint pain, in order: (1) time-of-day
confounding, (2) undiagnosed celiac as a chronic rather than per-meal effect, (3) central pain
sensitization in a gut–brain-interaction cluster.** The first is free to check and nobody has checked
it.

### 🔬 One zebra worth naming, because it costs nothing

**Whipple's disease.** Rheumatic manifestations occur in **~60% of cases and precede the GI signs in
three-quarters of patients** — often as the *first* symptom, with GI disease appearing years later.
Diagnostic delay runs up to six years. Against her: it typically features weight loss, fever and
malabsorption, and overwhelmingly affects middle-aged men.

**Probability <0.5%. Cost of considering it: zero, if she has an endoscopy anyway.** Duodenal
biopsies taken for celiac can be **PAS-stained** on the same specimen. Do not order a Whipple workup
standalone — just say *"if I'm having duodenal biopsies, please include PAS staining."*

## 2. ⚠️ Problem 1 is not solved. It has been relabelled.

The previous draft called the allergy mechanism settled. It is not, and the honest accounting matters
because **declaring it solved is the identical epistemic move that produced "alpha-gal syndrome":
a confident label on a thin base.**

### What is genuinely strong

**Alpha-gal is excluded.** Alpha-gal IgE ≥0.1 kU/L has reported sensitivity 100% / specificity 92.3%;
roughly **2% of patients referred for AGS evaluation react despite negative alpha-gal IgE.** Two
negatives a year apart drives residual probability to **~2–3%.** This is the strongest conclusion in
the whole case.

**One shared cross-reactive epitope, rather than three independent allergies: ~85%.** Independent
pork sensitization with zero pork exposure would require occupational or inhalational contact.

### What is weaker than claimed

| Problem | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **⚠️ My "never eaten pork" argument did less work than I claimed** | I treated *"you cannot be sensitized by something you have never ingested"* as near-decisive proof of a non-oral route. **That was wrong.** It requires only **cross-reactivity**: any pan-mammalian epitope in **beef or lamb** — foods she *has* eaten — generates pork IgE with zero pork exposure and no non-oral route at all. **Every candidate mechanism clears that bar.** The non-oral route is needed to explain something narrower |
| **What the pork value actually argues** | Not that pork IgE *exists*, but that pork is **highest (14.40) while beef is lowest (2.38)** — despite beef being her named trigger. Under a beef- or lamb-driven muscle-protein model you would expect the sensitizing species to be highest; beef being lowest is discordant. Under pork-cat syndrome, **pork > beef is the signature pattern.** That hierarchy is the real evidence, and it still favors albumin |
| **The clinical correlate is missing where the number is highest** | Her highest value has never been clinically tested; her main trigger food has her weakest number |
| **Cat IgE has never actually been measured** | The cat history is *patient recall* about a pet she no longer has. **e1, Fel d 1 and Fel d 2 have never been run.** A load-bearing assumption has been presented as a finding |
| **"Cat" was assumed because cats are famous** | Guinea pig, rabbit, hamster, dog and horse albumins all cross-react in the same family. Nobody asked about other pets, raw pet food handling, or pig contact |
| **⭐ Beef 2.38 sits inside the interference band** | Cross-reactive carbohydrate determinant interference on ImmunoCAP's cellulose solid phase **"predominantly affects low-positive IgE results (0.35–3.00 kUA/L)."** ~35% of sensitized patients carry CCD-IgE; **17% of CCD-positive sera gave false positives.** This exonerates pork 14.40 and lamb 10.20 — far above the band — but **the one number connecting lab to clinic is the least trustworthy in the panel** |
| **⭐ Total IgE is a Problem 1 test, and the last draft filed it under Problem 2** | Every value here is uninterpretable without it |

### The total IgE arithmetic — one cheap test rewrites all three values

High total IgE produces multiple weak positives to non-cross-reactive allergens. The
**specific/total IgE ratio ≥0.002** discriminates true reactivity from nonspecific binding.

| If total IgE is… | Pork ratio | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 kU/L | 0.24 | Dominant, near-monoclonal response. The albumin story becomes very strong |
| 500 kU/L | 0.029 | Meaningful — but beef 2.38 (0.005) is marginal |
| **3,000 kU/L** | 0.005 | Polysensitized atopic background. **Beef 2.38 = 0.0008 — below threshold. Noise.** |

### ⭐ The cleanest falsification test — and the odds it fails

**nFel d 2 (cat serum albumin, e220).** In pork-cat syndrome, **cat albumin is the primary
sensitizer** and the meat IgE is the epiphenomenon. **A negative Fel d 2 falsifies the hypothesis
outright.** Nothing else in the workup is that clean.

**Probability it returns <0.35: roughly 40%.** Most cat-allergic people are Fel d 1-dominant, Fel d 2
sensitization is a minority phenomenon, she lost the cats 3–4 years ago and albumin IgE decays with
removed exposure, and her clinical picture is beef and lamb, not pork. Pushing the other way: a
14.40 pork IgE with zero pork exposure is genuinely hard to explain without an animal-dander source.

**Best value: one tube, five analytes** — e220 (Fel d 2), e94 (Fel d 1), e1 (cat), Bos d 6, and
**total IgE**. That either confirms the formulation or destroys it in a single draw.

**Probability the meat allergy is albumin-mediated: ~48%.** Not the 70–80% originally claimed, and
not the 40% I posted after the doneness answer — see §0b for why that was an over-correction. Full
mechanism table below.

## 6. HaT and MCAS — right priors, wrong conclusions

**Hereditary alpha-tryptasemia.** The prior is fine (4–6% population, ~1/3 symptomatic, 17% in
idiopathic anaphylaxis cohorts). **But HaT is not a treatment target.** No therapy exists; it
modestly raises anaphylaxis severity risk, which the epinephrine prescription already mitigates. Its
real value is *stopping the search*. **Baseline tryptase first; TPSAB1 genotyping only if tryptase
>8 ng/mL.** Ranking it third on an *action* list was wrong even though the prior was right.

**MCAS.** The consensus (Vienna/Valent) criteria require all three of: typical multisystem symptoms,
an **event-related tryptase rise of baseline × 1.2 + 2 ng/mL**, and response to mast-cell-directed
therapy. **She has never had a baseline tryptase, so she cannot meet criterion 2 by any version — and
can never meet it retrospectively.**

- P(meets consensus criteria): **<5%**
- P(would be labelled MCAS under the loose Afrin criteria): **>60%** — which tells you about the
  criteria, not about her

> **⭐ And here is the lesson the previous draft noted but failed to draw:** her existing "serious
> histamine problem" label is a **loose-criteria label applied with zero testing.** It is the direct
> ancestor of the alpha-gal error, produced by the same machinery. Adding a genetic label before a
> single functional test would be the third iteration of the same mistake.

## 7. ⚠️ There was no treatment plan. Only tests.

Ten years, and the previous document proposed **not one therapeutic intervention.**

**Amitriptyline 10 mg at night** treats migraine, functional GI pain, and sleep — **three of her four
symptom domains with one cheap tablet**, and the answer is known in six weeks. It is grade-A evidence
for IBS (ATLANTIS, *Lancet* 2023, NNT ≈ 6) and standard migraine prophylaxis. See `INTERIM-PLAN.md`
for the full set — gut-directed hypnotherapy, GI-specific CBT and soluble fiber all carry grade-A
evidence and **she has never been offered any of them.**

**And on mood:** anxiety and depression are highly prevalent in disorders of gut–brain interaction
and are **bidirectionally causal, not merely reactive.** Not asking is a clinical omission, not
kindness. It is a treatable thing, not an insult — and skipping it because it sounds dismissive
leaves a treatable contributor untreated for another decade.

## 8. The workup — the ~$150 version first

### Tier 1: the panel a skeptical academic gastroenterologist would actually order

**One draw. It excludes everything dangerous and everything fixable.**

| Test | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **tTG-IgA + total serum IgA** | ⏳ Celiac. **On gluten. Before any diet change** |
| **CBC + ferritin + CRP** | ⭐ Ferritin <30 in a menstruating woman with a decade of gut symptoms is the **highest-probability abnormal result in this case** — and it is a finding, a treatment, and a reason to think harder about celiac, all from one number. Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant, so CRP must accompany it |
| **TSH** | Never drawn |
| **B12, folate, vitamin D** | Deficiency states after a decade of symptoms and a year of elimination |
| **⭐ Fecal calprotectin** | ~$30, and it **triages microscopic colitis, eosinophilic gastroenteritis and enteropathic arthritis at once.** Under 50 with normal CRP and ferritin and no red flags → stop looking for inflammation. Over 150 → colonoscopy, and you are hunting Crohn's, not zebras. *(It will not exclude microscopic colitis — calprotectin is often normal there — but it triages everything else)* |
| **One stool GI PCR panel** | Giardia and friends, once |
| **⭐ Total serum IgE** | A **Problem 1** test — see §2. Without it, none of her meat values can be interpreted |

**If serology is equivocal, two techniques go further — both specialist:**

- **CAP-inhibition.** Pre-incubate her serum with cat extract, then re-run pork, beef and lamb. **A
  substantial fall in pork IgE proves the cat→pork route directly; no fall kills the pork-cat framing
  outright.** This is the experiment that founded the syndrome. Research labs only — not a catalogue
  test at Quest, Labcorp, Mayo or Viracor.
- **Basophil activation test.** Measures *functional* reactivity rather than sensitization, and
  uniquely **can be run against different preparations of the same allergen — native BSA versus
  heated BSA, raw versus cooked meat extract.** That is precisely the discriminator this case needs,
  without an oral challenge. About eight US labs run BAT under CLIA/CAP; **Amerimmune** (Fairfax, VA)
  has a validated food-allergy BAT. Custom allergen preparation must be arranged in advance.

**A sobering note on the whole field:** in the original published US pork-cat cohort, **confirmatory
food challenges were performed in 0 of 8 patients.** The syndrome's evidence base rests on
unchallenged history. A physician-supervised graded challenge — well-done beef one day, rare beef
another — remains the only thing that settles the doneness question, and it requires epinephrine in
hand first.
- **8 a.m. serum cortisol** — low prior, catastrophic if missed. ⚠️ If a naturopathic **salivary
  cortisol "adrenal stress index"** was ever run, set it aside: it is not validated for diagnosing
  adrenal insufficiency
- **ANA, anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB, RF** — ⚠️ **only bundled with a specific sicca question.** ANA at
  1:80 is positive in **13–20% of healthy women**; a low-titre positive here buys a rheumatology
  referral and years of anxiety for nothing. Be ready to explain a weak positive *before* drawing it
- **HLA-B27, CRP during a flare** — only if the joint pain turns out to have inflammatory features
- **Copper + ceruloplasmin** — only if she is taking zinc. Zinc-induced copper deficiency causes an
  **irreversible myelopathy** and is a real iatrogenic risk in a year of naturopathic supplementation

### Tier 3: endoscopy — genuinely conditional

⚠️ **The previous draft called combined EGD + ileocolonoscopy "the single highest-yield action." That
was probably wrong for this patient.** She has **no documented red flags**: no weight loss, no
anemia, no rectal bleeding, no fever, no nocturnal symptoms, no recorded family history — and no
recorded age. Guidelines do not support bidirectional endoscopy in a red-flag-negative patient under
about 45.

**Scope if:** calprotectin is elevated, ferritin comes back low with no other explanation, tTG is
positive, an alarm feature emerges, or her age puts her in a screening or higher-risk band.

**If she is scoped, the biopsy protocol is not optional** — celiac, eosinophilic gastritis and
duodenitis, microscopic colitis and disaccharidase deficiency **all have normal-appearing mucosa**,
and "scope looked normal, no biopsies taken" answers nothing and burns the procedure:

| Site | Number | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duodenal **bulb** | ≥2, **separate pot** | Ultra-short celiac |
| **Distal** duodenum | ≥4 | Celiac, Marsh grading |
| Duodenum, extra piece | 1, **SNAP-FROZEN** | Disaccharidase assay — must be arranged in advance, impossible on fixed tissue |
| Gastric antrum + body | 2 + 2 | Eosinophilic gastritis (≥30 eos/hpf), *H. pylori* |
| Esophagus prox + distal | 2 + 2 | EoE (≥15 eos/hpf) — take these even without dysphagia |
| **Right** colon | ≥4, **separate pot** | Microscopic colitis — right-sided yield is materially higher |
| **Left** colon | ≥4, **separate pot** | Microscopic colitis |
| Terminal ileum | ≥2 | Crohn's — and the classic cause of secondary bile acid malabsorption |

**Conditions:** still eating gluten · **off PPI 3–4 weeks** (a PPI resolves esophageal and gastric
eosinophilia and produces a falsely normal read) · off NSAIDs.

### On bile acid diarrhea, the pragmatic answer

**Don't test it — treat it.** SeHCAT is unavailable in the US and the fecal collection requires
100–150 g of fat per day for three days, which is a hard ask for someone with a meat allergy and a
year of restrictive eating. **Colesevelam for a fortnight costs almost nothing and answers the
question before a nuclear medicine department returns a call.**

⚠️ With the caveat that an early response does not *confirm* BAD — Schiepatti 2024 found initial
cholestyramine response was **similar in SeHCAT-positive and SeHCAT-negative patients.** It is a
therapeutic trial, not a diagnostic one. And if BAD is real, look for its **cause** — celiac,
microscopic colitis and ileal Crohn's all produce secondary BAD.

## 9. ⛔ Do not order

Each of these has a **positive rate far above its true prevalence**, which means it manufactures new
wrong labels — **which is precisely this patient's presenting problem.**

| Test | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| **SIBO/IMO breath test** | P(true SIBO explains a 10-year multisystem illness) **<5%**. P(the test returns "positive") **30–40%**. High positive rate, near-zero information content. **In a patient whose problem is a decade of accumulated wrong labels, this is the single most likely test here to manufacture a new false explanation and stall the workup for another two years** |
| **Urinary 5-HIAA** | Carcinoid requires flushing (absent) and secretory diarrhea persisting with fasting (absent). Small-bowel NET incidence ~1/100,000/yr. In this prevalence setting, with dietary interference, it generates more false positives than true diagnoses |
| **Serum DAO / histamine assays** | Not validated diagnostics for histamine intolerance |
| **IgG / IgG4 food sensitivity panels, ALCAT, MRT** | Measure exposure and tolerance, not disease. A primary engine of unnecessary restriction — and she is already inside an ecosystem that sells them |
| **Intestinal-permeability "leaky gut" testing** | No validated clinical utility |
| **Untargeted broad food IgE panels** | Guarantee more clinically irrelevant positives in a patient who already carries one wrong label |
| **Hair mineral analysis, urine mycotoxin panels** | No validity |
| **Provocative OGTT for "reactive hypoglycemia"** | **25% of subjects have hypoglycemia-like symptoms with no significant glucose decline.** Insist on Whipple's triad instead — symptoms, a documented low glucose *at the time*, and relief on correction |
| **MALS / mesenteric imaging** | Not without weight loss, which is the cardinal feature and which she does not have. Celiac artery compression is a common **incidental** finding; imaging now risks an incidentaloma that generates surgery she does not need |

| **TPSAB1 genotyping before a baseline tryptase** | Wrong order. The cheap test is the rule-out |
| **PEG/polysorbate skin testing as a screen** | 12.7% positivity in screening cohorts; utility openly debated. Only if excipient triangulation points there |

## 12. The bread question — the right order of operations

**Only after celiac serology has returned.**

Bread contains gluten, **fructans**, amylase-trypsin inhibitors, yeast and fermentation products, and
often added sugar alcohols and emulsifiers. **"Bread causes X" identifies bread, not gluten.**

**The literature points away from gluten.** In blinded challenge, most self-identified
gluten-sensitive people do not reproduce symptoms on gluten; Skodje et al. (*Gastroenterology* 2018)
found **fructan produced significantly higher symptom scores than gluten, and gluten did not differ
from placebo.** *(Recalled and not verified against full text — check before quoting.)*

> ### ⭐ The sourdough discriminator
>
> Genuine **long-fermented sourdough has substantially reduced fructan content** — fermentation
> consumes them — **while retaining full gluten.**
>
> - Tolerates long-fermented sourdough but not commercial fast-yeast bread → **the trigger is
>   fructans, not gluten**
> - Reacts equally to both → gluten and ATIs stay in play
>
> **Second discriminator: onion and garlic.** Both high-fructan and entirely gluten-free. If they
> trigger her too, it is FODMAPs.
>
> Free, delicious, and close to decisive.

**Structured n-of-1, not open elimination.** Two-week baseline, then reintroduce in discriminating
order: (a) long-fermented sourdough — gluten yes, fructan low; (b) gluten-free bread plus an
onion/garlic load — fructan yes, gluten no; (c) plain gluten-free bread — neither. Use a
**pre-specified joint score and a fixed lag window**, or every fluctuation gets attributed. **Blind
the challenges with a partner's help if at all possible** — unblinded, expect a strong nocebo signal.

**On the joint claim honestly:** celiac genuinely causes arthralgia. Beyond that, there is no
well-powered trial showing wheat withdrawal improves joint symptoms in non-celiac patients.
**P(a genuine wheat-specific mechanism drives her joint pain) ≈ 10%. P(the association is confounded)
≈ 60%+** — bread is a daily staple, so "I ate bread on a bad day" is nearly guaranteed, and she
formed the belief in the past year while under care that has been assigning her food explanations
for a decade.

**And on SAAT, in the clinical letter, plainly:** it has no evidence base, and "you no longer have a
gluten allergy" from a laser device **is not a test result** and must not be used to justify skipping
celiac serology.

## 13. The discipline point

The error that produced the alpha-gal label was **over-unification**. The error this document nearly
repeated was over-unification one level down — splitting one label into two, then hunting a single
elegant diagnosis inside the second.

**She does not need a thirteenth explanation. She has had ten years of them.** What she needs is two
prescriptions, one blood draw, one honest history, and a treatment trial — and the discipline to
accept that the answer is most likely **several ordinary things at once.**
