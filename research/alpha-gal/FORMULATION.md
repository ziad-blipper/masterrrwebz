# Unified Formulation

**What is actually going on.** This document supersedes the "leading candidates" section of
CASE-RECONSTRUCTION.md and is the current top-level answer to the question.

*August 2026. Research synthesis for discussion with treating clinicians. Not a diagnosis and not
medical advice. Every test named here must be ordered and interpreted by a physician.*

---

## The formulation in one paragraph

She has **two separate illnesses that were merged into one wrong label**, and the merger is the
central iatrogenic fact of this case. Illness one is a **genuine, immediate, IgE-mediated allergy to
mammalian serum albumin**, cross-sensitized from decades of cat exposure — mechanistically solved,
needing epinephrine and two confirmatory blood tests. Illness two is a **decade of GI-predominant
chronic illness that has never been investigated at all** — no endoscopy, no colonoscopy, no celiac
serology, no stool studies, no tryptase, in ten years. Because every symptom was attributed to the
food allergy, the chronic illness was never worked up. Removing the wrong label does not treat her.
**Investigating the second illness is the entire remaining task.**

## 1. What the four newest answers changed

| Answer | What it rules **out** | What it rules **in** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gradual onset, no clear starting event** | Post-infectious IBS (requires an index gastroenteritis) · post-surgical adhesions · acute-onset mast cell disease · a single toxic exposure | Celiac · endometriosis · slow autoimmune disease · hypermobility spectrum · bile acid diarrhea · disorders of gut–brain interaction — all of which creep |
| **No nocturnal diarrhea; loose stool fluctuating with normal, daytime, random** | Removes the single highest-specificity organic red flag. **Downgrades microscopic colitis**, which is classically nocturnal and watery | Bile acid diarrhea (postprandial, daytime, urgent) · IBS-M/D · carbohydrate malabsorption |
| **Yes — she reacted to cats** | — | **The pork-cat mechanism.** This was a *pre-registered prediction* of the albumin hypothesis before the answer was given, and it held |
| **The "histamine problem" was never based on any test** | Nothing — that is the point | **The largest single gap in ten years of care.** A named diagnosis driving a year of treatment, with zero objective basis |

### The cat answer deserves a note

The albumin hypothesis required a **non-oral sensitization route**, because her highest antibody
(pork, 14.40) is to a food she has never eaten and sensitization requires exposure. The hypothesis
therefore predicted, in advance, that she should have clinical cat reactivity. She does. That is a
prediction that could have failed and didn't — which is worth considerably more than a fact gathered
after the model was built to fit it.

## 2. The failed year is the most useful piece of evidence in the case

She completed **a full year of complete mammalian meat and dairy elimination with essentially no
improvement** in the chronic illness.

This is usually filed as a disappointment. It should be filed as **a negative experiment with real
inferential power.** It does not mean nothing was found; it means something specific was excluded.

**The rule it generates:** any candidate diagnosis for the chronic illness must survive a year of
mammalian elimination. Only two categories do.

| Category | Members |
| --- | --- |
| **Diet-independent** — the food didn't matter | Bile acid diarrhea · microscopic colitis · endometriosis · hereditary alpha-tryptasemia · thyroid disease · autoimmune disease · structural/pelvic causes |
| **Depends on a food she did NOT eliminate** | **Gluten** — she is still eating bread, and she is the one who noticed the bread association |

Everything else — the histamine-diet framing, the mast-cell-from-meat framing, the dairy framing —
has already been tested for a year and failed. **This is why the differential below looks the way it
does.** It is not a shopping list; it is what remains standing after the evidence she already
generated.

## 4. Models A and B are not competing — they are sequential

Two unifying models were stress-tested separately:

- **Model A — celiac as the root.** Strongest *primary* hypothesis: uniquely explains the
  bread–joint association, and is the only candidate that is **disease-modifying** rather than
  symptom-managing.
- **Model B — hereditary alpha-tryptasemia as the amplifier.** Strongest *modifier* hypothesis:
  explains anaphylaxis severity and multi-drug reactivity, neither of which celiac can.

**The bridge (AJG, Feb 2026):** HaT was found in **12.3% of celiac patients with persisting symptoms
despite a gluten-free diet**, and **every HaT-positive celiac patient had ongoing symptoms** with
increased duodenal mast cells.

**So do not pick.** Both tests are on the same blood draw, both cost little, and the combination is a
described clinical entity. The two-hit reading — a chronic mucosal disease plus a genetic
mast-cell amplifier — fits her presentation better than either model alone.

### The honest objection to Model A, stated plainly

The DAO chain (celiac → villous atrophy → loss of brush-border diamine oxidase → histamine
intolerance) requires **severe** atrophy to abolish DAO. Severe atrophy usually produces nutritional
deficiencies and positive serology. **The hypothesis needs the lesion to be simultaneously severe and
silent.** That is possible but it is the weak link, and it should be said out loud rather than
smoothed over. The celiac test is still the first thing to draw — because the *diagnosis* stands or
falls on its own, independent of whether the DAO chain is real.

## 6. The one blood draw

Everything below can be drawn on the same day, in the same visit.

| Test | Why | ⚠️ The trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **tTG-IgA + total serum IgA** | Celiac | **Must be on gluten.** tTG-IgA falls ~50% within 30–60 days off it. Total IgA is non-negotiable — IgA deficiency is 10–15× more common in celiac and makes a negative tTG-IgA meaningless |
| **HLA-DQ2 / DQ8** | Diet-independent celiac insurance; NPV >99% | Order regardless — it is the only celiac test that still works if she has already cut gluten |
| **Baseline serum tryptase** | The histamine question, finally measured | Draw **≥24 h from any reaction**. ≥8 ng/mL → reflex to TPSAB1 copy number by ddPCR |
| **Total serum IgE** | Contextualizes every specific IgE she has ever had | — |
| **Fel d 2** (cat albumin, e220) | ⭐ Should be her **highest** value if the formulation is right | **Order directly.** Reflex cat panels only run components if whole-cat IgE ≥0.35, and she has been cat-free for years — the reflex would silently cancel the most important test in her workup |
| **Bos d 6** (bovine albumin, e204) | The gradient direction settles the mechanism | — |
| Repeat alpha-gal IgE | Formal closure, paired with the total IgE | Not because it will be positive — because a diagnosis closed properly stays closed |
| CBC, ferritin, B12, vitamin D | A decade of GI symptoms + a year of elimination | Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant — read it with CRP |
| TSH (+ free T4) | Both hypo- and hyperthyroidism change bowel habit | — |
| CRP, ESR | The joint symptoms deserve one objective number | — |

**Stool, same week:** fecal calprotectin · fecal bile acids (48-h quantitative, or serum 7αC4) ·
*Giardia* antigen and a GI pathogen panel.

**Do not order:** serum DAO (not a validated diagnostic) · IgG/IgG4 food sensitivity panels (they
measure exposure and tolerance, not disease — and they are the main engine of unnecessary
restriction) · hair mineral analysis · urine mycotoxin panels · untargeted broad food IgE panels
(they guarantee more clinically irrelevant positives in a patient who already carries one wrong
label).

## 7. The one endoscopy

**Combined EGD + ileocolonoscopy with protocolized biopsies.** This is the single highest-yield
action in the entire case — it addresses five of the eight candidates in one procedure.

### Conditions — these decide whether the procedure is worth doing

- **Still eating gluten.** Off gluten, the duodenal histology normalizes and the celiac question
  cannot be answered.
- **Off any PPI for 3–4 weeks.** A PPI can resolve esophageal and gastric eosinophilia and produce a
  falsely normal read.
- **Off NSAIDs**, which cause their own mucosal changes.

### The biopsy protocol

| Site | Number | Looking for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duodenal **bulb** | ≥2, **separate pot** | Ultra-short celiac — missed if pooled with distal |
| **Distal** duodenum | ≥4 | Celiac (Marsh grading) |
| Duodenum — extra | 1 piece **SNAP-FROZEN** | **Disaccharidase assay** (sucrase-isomaltase, lactase). Must be arranged *in advance* — it cannot be done on formalin-fixed tissue |
| Gastric antrum | ≥2 | Eosinophilic gastritis (≥30 eos/hpf), *H. pylori* |
| Gastric body | ≥2 | Same |
| Esophagus proximal + distal | 2 + 2 | Eosinophilic esophagitis (≥15 eos/hpf) — **take these even without dysphagia** |
| **Right** colon | ≥4, **separate pot** | Microscopic colitis — right-sided yield is materially higher |
| **Left** colon | ≥4, **separate pot** | Microscopic colitis |
| Terminal ileum | ≥2 | Crohn's, ileal disease causing secondary bile acid malabsorption |

> ### ⚠️ The failure mode to name explicitly
>
> **"Scope looked normal, no biopsies taken."** Celiac, eosinophilic gastritis, eosinophilic
> duodenitis, microscopic colitis, and disaccharidase deficiency **all have normal-appearing
> mucosa.** A visually normal endoscopy without protocolized biopsies answers nothing and burns the
> procedure. Hand the endoscopist this list.

## 9. The three things that do not wait

1. **Epinephrine, two auto-injectors.** Her reactions involve three organ systems and meet the
   criteria for anaphylaxis. Specific IgE level **does not predict severity**, so a decade of
   moderate reactions gives no assurance about the next one. This is independent of every diagnostic
   question in this document and is the most urgent item in the case.
2. **tTG-IgA + total serum IgA, drawn before any gluten removal.** The only test here with a
   deadline.
3. **Therefore: do not go gluten-free until #2 is drawn.** The instinct after reading this will be to
   cut bread immediately. That instinct would destroy the ability to diagnose the thing it is
   reacting to. Two weeks of patience protects a diagnosis that lasts a lifetime.

## 11. The discipline point

The error that produced the alpha-gal label was **over-unification** — forcing every symptom into one
box. The risk now is doing it again with a better-sounding box.

**"Pork-cat syndrome" explains her meat allergy. It does not explain her illness.** Nothing in this
document should be read as claiming otherwise. It is entirely possible — likely, even — that the
chronic illness has **two or three** contributing causes rather than one elegant answer. A
formulation that resolves into "bile acid diarrhea plus central sensitization plus a mast cell
trait" would be less satisfying than a single unifying diagnosis and considerably more likely to be
true.
