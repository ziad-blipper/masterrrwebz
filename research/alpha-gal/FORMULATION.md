## ⚡ The two things that matter, before anything else

Everything else in this document is discussion. **These two are actions, they are cheap, and neither
depends on resolving a single item in the differential.**

### 1. Epinephrine — two auto-injectors and a written anaphylaxis action plan

She has documented **multi-organ immediate reactions** — oral mucosa, plus presyncopal dizziness,
plus GI — to a food she still encounters, and she has **no epinephrine.**

Whether the mechanism turns out to be cat albumin, bovine albumin, gelatin, or three independent
sensitizations is **therapeutically irrelevant to this prescription.** Arguing about which albumin
while she carries no adrenaline is arguing about the log format during an outage.

> #### ⚠️ And there is a specific, foreseeable route to a severe reaction that nobody has named
>
> She was told that **three rounds of SAAT (soft-laser "allergy antidote" acupuncture) cured her
> gluten allergy.** If she — or that practitioner — draws the obvious next inference, that SAAT can
> also clear her **meat** allergy, **she will deliberately eat steak to prove it.** With no
> epinephrine in the house.
>
> That is not a hypothetical. It is the predictable consequence of the belief she has already been
> given, and it is an independent reason the prescription is urgent **today**.

### 2. tTG-IgA + total serum IgA — drawn before she stops eating bread

⏳ **This is the only genuinely unrecoverable window in the entire case.**

She is still eating gluten. The moment she starts a gluten-free trial — which her own bread belief
makes very likely, and which *reading this document will accelerate* — tTG-IgA falls and duodenal
histology normalizes over weeks. She then becomes undiagnosable without a formal 6–8 week gluten
challenge that she will not want to do, and may spend the rest of her life gluten-free without ever
knowing whether she needed to be, and without the care a real celiac diagnosis triggers: bone
density, nutrient monitoring, first-degree-relative screening, associated autoimmune surveillance.

**Total IgA is mandatory, not optional.** Selective IgA deficiency is ~1:500 in the general
population and 10–15× enriched in celiac; a lone tTG-IgA in an IgA-deficient patient is a false
negative. If IgA-deficient → DGP-IgG or tTG-IgG.

**Do not lead with HLA-DQ2/DQ8.** Negative predictive value is ~99%, but **30–40% of the general
population carries DQ2/DQ8**, so a positive tells you nothing. It is the fallback for someone
already off gluten, not a first-line test.

> ### 🛑 The single most important instruction in this document
>
> **Do not change her diet until the celiac result returns.** Not gluten, not anything. Two weeks of
> patience protects a diagnosis that lasts a lifetime.

## 0b. ⚠️ Four answers, and a prediction that partly failed

| Question | Answer | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Doneness** | **Mixed — reactions across cooking levels** | ⚠️ **The albumin hypothesis is weakened.** Serum albumin is thermolabile. Reactions to well-cooked meat point to **heat-stable** allergens — beef **myoglobin**, **Bos d 13** (myosin light chains) — or to a non-albumin mechanism. **Probability the meat allergy is albumin-mediated drops from 55–60% to roughly 40%** |
| **Alcohol** | **She doesn't drink** | ✅ Cleanly eliminated as a cofactor, a migraine trigger, a diarrhea cause and a joint-pain cause. It also means her reactions occurred **without** the classic threshold-lowering cofactor — which makes them more concerning, not less |
| **Analgesic days** | **Under 10 per month** | ✅ **Medication-overuse headache is excluded.** A 40–60% conditional closed for free. It also weakens NSAID hypersensitivity as the explanation for her drug reactivity — she is not a high-exposure NSAID user |
| **⭐ Joint pain character** | **"Happens after food"** | 🔄 **This reframes the case.** Not morning stiffness, not mechanical. **Postprandial.** See below |

### What the doneness answer means, stated fairly

"Mixed" is weaker evidence than a clean "she reacted to a long-braised stew," and it may reflect
unreliable recall rather than a true absence of a heat gradient — restaurant doneness is
unverifiable and nobody was recording. But **I do not get to treat an inconvenient answer as noise
after calling the question decisive.** The honest reading:

- **Albumin remains the leading single mechanism, but it is no longer the confident one.**
- **Heat-stable beef allergens rise materially.** Myoglobin and Bos d 13 survive 85 °C for two hours,
  and six beef protein fractions survive prolonged heating.
- This also **retroactively vindicates the earlier correction** in `CASE-RECONSTRUCTION.md` §3 —
  "just cook it well done" was never a safe plan, and now there is history suggesting it does not
  work for her.
- **The Fel d 2 test matters more than ever.** It is now the only thing that can settle this, and it
  already carried a ~40% chance of coming back negative.

## 0c. ⭐ The postprandial pattern — the most useful new observation in the case

Her joint pain **happens after food.** That single word changes the shape of the problem.

Not inflammatory (no morning stiffness). Not mechanical (not use-related). **Meal-linked.** And if
the joint pain is postprandial, the obvious question is whether **everything else is too** — the
nausea, the abdominal pain, the loose stool, the headache.

If it is, she does not have four unrelated chronic symptoms. **She has one postprandial multi-system
symptom complex**, and that is a far more tractable object than what has been described for ten
years.

### ❓ The question this generates — and it is now the most important one

> **Do her GI symptoms, headache and joint aching all cluster after meals — and how long after?**
>
> And: **after *all* meals, or only some?** If only some, which — and is size, fat content or
> carbohydrate load the variable, rather than any specific food?

### What a postprandial multi-system complex can be

| Candidate | Fit |
| --- | --- |
| **⭐ Food-triggered mast cell mediator release** | **The one that would finally make the "histamine problem" label testable.** A postprandial multi-system complex is exactly what that label was gesturing at — and it has never once been measured |
| **Postprandial distress syndrome** (Rome IV functional dyspepsia) | Postprandial fullness, early satiety, nausea. A positive diagnosis with real treatments |
| **Postprandial hypotension / dysautonomia** | Explains meal-linked **dizziness** specifically — and she has dizziness |
| **Bile acid diarrhea** | Classically **postprandial**. This partially resurrects it from the demotion in §5 — though it still explains neither the joints nor the headache |
| **Carbohydrate malabsorption** | Postprandial bloating, pain, loose stool. Doesn't reach the joints |
| **Celiac** | Fits the bread–joint observation directly |

### ⭐ The test that follows, and it is the best idea in this document

**A postprandial tryptase.**

She has a "serious histamine problem" that has never been tested, and the reason is that **nobody
ever drew a baseline** — so no acute value could ever be interpreted. But now there is a
*predictable* trigger window.

1. Draw a **baseline serum tryptase** on a symptom-free day, ≥24 h from any reaction.
2. Then draw an **acute tryptase within 1–4 hours of a meal that produces her symptoms.**
3. Apply the consensus rule: a rise exceeding **baseline × 1.2 + 2 ng/mL** is objective evidence of
   systemic mast cell degranulation.

**Ten years of symptoms have generated zero tryptase measurements.** This converts an untested label
into a yes-or-no answer for about $100, and it is the single most direct route to settling the
question she has been managed on for a year.

⚠️ Two caveats to attach: the formula proves **that** mast cells degranulated, not **why** — it
cannot separate IgE from non-IgE mechanisms. And a flat tryptase does not exclude a reaction,
particularly a mild one.

## 1. The formulation

She has **two problems that were merged into one wrong label**, and the merger is the central
iatrogenic fact of this case.

**Problem 1 — an acute, immediate, IgE-mediated allergy to mammalian meat.** The leading mechanism is
**mammalian serum albumin**, cross-sensitized from decades of cat exposure. **This is a
better-specified hypothesis, not a solved problem** — see §2.

**Problem 2 — a decade of multisystem chronic illness that has never been investigated.** No
endoscopy, no colonoscopy, no celiac serology, no stool studies, no tryptase, no total IgE, no
ferritin, in ten years. Because every symptom was attributed to the food allergy, **the chronic
illness was never worked up at all.**

Removing the wrong label does not treat her. Investigating Problem 2 — cheaply, and in the right
order — is the remaining task.

## 2. ⚠️ Problem 1 is not solved. It has been relabelled.

The previous draft called the allergy mechanism settled. It is not, and the honest accounting matters
because **declaring it solved is the identical epistemic move that produced "alpha-gal syndrome":
a confident label on a thin base.**

### What is genuinely strong

**Alpha-gal is excluded.** Alpha-gal IgE ≥0.1 kU/L has reported sensitivity 100% / specificity 92.3%;
roughly **2% of patients referred for AGS evaluation react despite negative alpha-gal IgE.** Two
negatives a year apart drives residual probability to **~2–3%.** This is the strongest conclusion in
the whole case.

**One shared cross-reactive epitope, rather than three independent allergies: ~85%.** Independent
pork sensitization with zero pork exposure would require occupational or inhalational contact.

### What is weaker than claimed

| Problem | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **The hierarchies are inverted** | Pork is her **highest** IgE (14.40) and has **no clinical correlate** — she has never eaten it. Beef is her **lowest** (2.38) and is her **main clinical trigger** (steak). The formulation leans on the number with no clinical correlate |
| **Cat IgE has never actually been measured** | The cat history is *patient recall* about a pet she no longer has. **e1, Fel d 1 and Fel d 2 have never been run.** A load-bearing assumption has been presented as a finding |
| **"Cat" was assumed because cats are famous** | Guinea pig, rabbit, hamster, dog and horse albumins all cross-react in the same family. Nobody asked about other pets, raw pet food handling, or pig contact |
| **⭐ Beef 2.38 sits inside the interference band** | Cross-reactive carbohydrate determinant interference on ImmunoCAP's cellulose solid phase **"predominantly affects low-positive IgE results (0.35–3.00 kUA/L)."** ~35% of sensitized patients carry CCD-IgE; **17% of CCD-positive sera gave false positives.** This exonerates pork 14.40 and lamb 10.20 — far above the band — but **the one number connecting lab to clinic is the least trustworthy in the panel** |
| **⭐ Total IgE is a Problem 1 test, and the last draft filed it under Problem 2** | Every value here is uninterpretable without it |

### The total IgE arithmetic — one cheap test rewrites all three values

High total IgE produces multiple weak positives to non-cross-reactive allergens. The
**specific/total IgE ratio ≥0.002** discriminates true reactivity from nonspecific binding.

| If total IgE is… | Pork ratio | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 kU/L | 0.24 | Dominant, near-monoclonal response. The albumin story becomes very strong |
| 500 kU/L | 0.029 | Meaningful — but beef 2.38 (0.005) is marginal |
| **3,000 kU/L** | 0.005 | Polysensitized atopic background. **Beef 2.38 = 0.0008 — below threshold. Noise.** |

### ⭐ The cleanest falsification test — and the odds it fails

**nFel d 2 (cat serum albumin, e220).** In pork-cat syndrome, **cat albumin is the primary
sensitizer** and the meat IgE is the epiphenomenon. **A negative Fel d 2 falsifies the hypothesis
outright.** Nothing else in the workup is that clean.

**Probability it returns <0.35: roughly 40%.** Most cat-allergic people are Fel d 1-dominant, Fel d 2
sensitization is a minority phenomenon, she lost the cats 3–4 years ago and albumin IgE decays with
removed exposure, and her clinical picture is beef and lamb, not pork. Pushing the other way: a
14.40 pork IgE with zero pork exposure is genuinely hard to explain without an animal-dander source.

**Best value: one tube, five analytes** — e220 (Fel d 2), e94 (Fel d 1), e1 (cat), Bos d 6, and
**total IgE**. That either confirms the formulation or destroys it in a single draw.

**Probability the meat allergy is albumin-mediated: ~40%.** Not the 70–80% originally claimed, and
now below the 55–60% this section carried before the doneness answer came back mixed (§0b). **Heat-stable
beef allergens — myoglobin and Bos d 13 — are now a live competing mechanism**, and the Fel d 2 test
is the only thing that separates them.

## 6. HaT and MCAS — right priors, wrong conclusions

**Hereditary alpha-tryptasemia.** The prior is fine (4–6% population, ~1/3 symptomatic, 17% in
idiopathic anaphylaxis cohorts). **But HaT is not a treatment target.** No therapy exists; it
modestly raises anaphylaxis severity risk, which the epinephrine prescription already mitigates. Its
real value is *stopping the search*. **Baseline tryptase first; TPSAB1 genotyping only if tryptase
>8 ng/mL.** Ranking it third on an *action* list was wrong even though the prior was right.

**MCAS.** The consensus (Vienna/Valent) criteria require all three of: typical multisystem symptoms,
an **event-related tryptase rise of baseline × 1.2 + 2 ng/mL**, and response to mast-cell-directed
therapy. **She has never had a baseline tryptase, so she cannot meet criterion 2 by any version — and
can never meet it retrospectively.**

- P(meets consensus criteria): **<5%**
- P(would be labelled MCAS under the loose Afrin criteria): **>60%** — which tells you about the
  criteria, not about her

> **⭐ And here is the lesson the previous draft noted but failed to draw:** her existing "serious
> histamine problem" label is a **loose-criteria label applied with zero testing.** It is the direct
> ancestor of the alpha-gal error, produced by the same machinery. Adding a genetic label before a
> single functional test would be the third iteration of the same mistake.

Ten years, and the previous document proposed **not one therapeutic intervention.**

**Amitriptyline 10 mg at night** treats migraine, functional GI pain, and sleep — **three of her four

## 8. The workup — the ~$150 version first

### Tier 1: the panel a skeptical academic gastroenterologist would actually order

**One draw. It excludes everything dangerous and everything fixable.**

| Test | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **tTG-IgA + total serum IgA** | ⏳ Celiac. **On gluten. Before any diet change** |
| **CBC + ferritin + CRP** | ⭐ Ferritin <30 in a menstruating woman with a decade of gut symptoms is the **highest-probability abnormal result in this case** — and it is a finding, a treatment, and a reason to think harder about celiac, all from one number. Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant, so CRP must accompany it |
| **TSH** | Never drawn |
| **B12, folate, vitamin D** | Deficiency states after a decade of symptoms and a year of elimination |
| **⭐ Fecal calprotectin** | ~$30, and it **triages microscopic colitis, eosinophilic gastroenteritis and enteropathic arthritis at once.** Under 50 with normal CRP and ferritin and no red flags → stop looking for inflammation. Over 150 → colonoscopy, and you are hunting Crohn's, not zebras. *(It will not exclude microscopic colitis — calprotectin is often normal there — but it triages everything else)* |
| **One stool GI PCR panel** | Giardia and friends, once |
| **⭐ Total serum IgE** | A **Problem 1** test — see §2. Without it, none of her meat values can be interpreted |
| **Baseline serum tryptase** | ~$50, ten years overdue, and it closes or opens the entire mast cell branch |
| **Corrected calcium** | Already on any metabolic panel. **Primary hyperparathyroidism** — "bones, stones, abdominal groans" — is curable and almost never checked in a GI workup |

### Tier 2: conditional, not automatic

- **Fel d 2 (e220) + Fel d 1 + e1 + Bos d 6** — the allergy question. Same draw as above if the
  allergist agrees; see §2
- **8 a.m. serum cortisol** — low prior, catastrophic if missed. ⚠️ If a naturopathic **salivary
  cortisol "adrenal stress index"** was ever run, set it aside: it is not validated for diagnosing
  adrenal insufficiency
- **ANA, anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB, RF** — ⚠️ **only bundled with a specific sicca question.** ANA at
  1:80 is positive in **13–20% of healthy women**; a low-titre positive here buys a rheumatology
  referral and years of anxiety for nothing. Be ready to explain a weak positive *before* drawing it
- **HLA-B27, CRP during a flare** — only if the joint pain turns out to have inflammatory features
- **Copper + ceruloplasmin** — only if she is taking zinc. Zinc-induced copper deficiency causes an
  **irreversible myelopathy** and is a real iatrogenic risk in a year of naturopathic supplementation

### Tier 3: endoscopy — genuinely conditional

⚠️ **The previous draft called combined EGD + ileocolonoscopy "the single highest-yield action." That
was probably wrong for this patient.** She has **no documented red flags**: no weight loss, no
anemia, no rectal bleeding, no fever, no nocturnal symptoms, no recorded family history — and no
recorded age. Guidelines do not support bidirectional endoscopy in a red-flag-negative patient under
about 45.

**Scope if:** calprotectin is elevated, ferritin comes back low with no other explanation, tTG is
positive, an alarm feature emerges, or her age puts her in a screening or higher-risk band.

**If she is scoped, the biopsy protocol is not optional** — celiac, eosinophilic gastritis and
duodenitis, microscopic colitis and disaccharidase deficiency **all have normal-appearing mucosa**,
and "scope looked normal, no biopsies taken" answers nothing and burns the procedure:

| Site | Number | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duodenal **bulb** | ≥2, **separate pot** | Ultra-short celiac |
| **Distal** duodenum | ≥4 | Celiac, Marsh grading |
| Duodenum, extra piece | 1, **SNAP-FROZEN** | Disaccharidase assay — must be arranged in advance, impossible on fixed tissue |
| Gastric antrum + body | 2 + 2 | Eosinophilic gastritis (≥30 eos/hpf), *H. pylori* |
| Esophagus prox + distal | 2 + 2 | EoE (≥15 eos/hpf) — take these even without dysphagia |
| **Right** colon | ≥4, **separate pot** | Microscopic colitis — right-sided yield is materially higher |
| **Left** colon | ≥4, **separate pot** | Microscopic colitis |
| Terminal ileum | ≥2 | Crohn's — and the classic cause of secondary bile acid malabsorption |

**Conditions:** still eating gluten · **off PPI 3–4 weeks** (a PPI resolves esophageal and gastric
eosinophilia and produces a falsely normal read) · off NSAIDs.

### On bile acid diarrhea, the pragmatic answer

**Don't test it — treat it.** SeHCAT is unavailable in the US and the fecal collection requires
100–150 g of fat per day for three days, which is a hard ask for someone with a meat allergy and a
year of restrictive eating. **Colesevelam for a fortnight costs almost nothing and answers the
question before a nuclear medicine department returns a call.**

⚠️ With the caveat that an early response does not *confirm* BAD — Schiepatti 2024 found initial
cholestyramine response was **similar in SeHCAT-positive and SeHCAT-negative patients.** It is a
therapeutic trial, not a diagnostic one. And if BAD is real, look for its **cause** — celiac,
microscopic colitis and ileal Crohn's all produce secondary BAD.

## 12. The bread question — the right order of operations

**Only after celiac serology has returned.**

Bread contains gluten, **fructans**, amylase-trypsin inhibitors, yeast and fermentation products, and
often added sugar alcohols and emulsifiers. **"Bread causes X" identifies bread, not gluten.**

**The literature points away from gluten.** In blinded challenge, most self-identified
gluten-sensitive people do not reproduce symptoms on gluten; Skodje et al. (*Gastroenterology* 2018)
found **fructan produced significantly higher symptom scores than gluten, and gluten did not differ
from placebo.** *(Recalled and not verified against full text — check before quoting.)*

> ### ⭐ The sourdough discriminator
>
> Genuine **long-fermented sourdough has substantially reduced fructan content** — fermentation
> consumes them — **while retaining full gluten.**
>
> - Tolerates long-fermented sourdough but not commercial fast-yeast bread → **the trigger is
>   fructans, not gluten**
> - Reacts equally to both → gluten and ATIs stay in play
>
> **Second discriminator: onion and garlic.** Both high-fructan and entirely gluten-free. If they
> trigger her too, it is FODMAPs.
>
> Free, delicious, and close to decisive.

**Structured n-of-1, not open elimination.** Two-week baseline, then reintroduce in discriminating
order: (a) long-fermented sourdough — gluten yes, fructan low; (b) gluten-free bread plus an
onion/garlic load — fructan yes, gluten no; (c) plain gluten-free bread — neither. Use a
**pre-specified joint score and a fixed lag window**, or every fluctuation gets attributed. **Blind
the challenges with a partner's help if at all possible** — unblinded, expect a strong nocebo signal.

**On the joint claim honestly:** celiac genuinely causes arthralgia. Beyond that, there is no
well-powered trial showing wheat withdrawal improves joint symptoms in non-celiac patients.
**P(a genuine wheat-specific mechanism drives her joint pain) ≈ 10%. P(the association is confounded)
≈ 60%+** — bread is a daily staple, so "I ate bread on a bad day" is nearly guaranteed, and she
formed the belief in the past year while under care that has been assigning her food explanations
for a decade.

**And on SAAT, in the clinical letter, plainly:** it has no evidence base, and "you no longer have a
gluten allergy" from a laser device **is not a test result** and must not be used to justify skipping
celiac serology.

## 13. The discipline point

The error that produced the alpha-gal label was **over-unification**. The error this document nearly
repeated was over-unification one level down — splitting one label into two, then hunting a single
elegant diagnosis inside the second.

**She does not need a thirteenth explanation. She has had ten years of them.** What she needs is two
prescriptions, one blood draw, one honest history, and a treatment trial — and the discipline to
accept that the answer is most likely **several ordinary things at once.**
