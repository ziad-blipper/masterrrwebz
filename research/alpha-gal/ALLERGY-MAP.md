# The Full Allergy Picture — Meat, Egg and Plants

*September 2026. Research synthesis for discussion with her allergist. Not a diagnosis.*

---

## The one-line answer

**No single protein explains mammalian meat + egg + plants.** They are unrelated families.

But there is a unifying **phenotype**, and it is the most useful sentence to say to an allergist:

> **She reacts to heat-labile, digestion-labile proteins at the oral mucosa.** Raw and
> under-cooked trigger; thoroughly cooked is tolerated; symptoms start in the mouth and often stop
> there.

That is not one molecule. It is at least two separate IgE responses that happen to share a
physical property — **fragility**. Serum albumin (confirmed) plus, most likely, a **pollen-food**
axis. Egg is the branch point.

**Her well-done beef tolerance is the anchor.** If the same raw-bad / cooked-fine rule holds for
her plants and her egg, that is strong convergent evidence — and it costs nothing to check.

---

## 1. ⚠️ Correction: egg yolk is worth testing, not assuming

Last round I flagged **Gal d 5 (chicken serum albumin, in egg yolk)** as possibly the same allergy
extending to birds, and said it "moves to the front." **That was too strong.** The numbers say
otherwise.

### The sequence identity ladder — this is the decisive figure

Cat albumin (Fel d 2) compared with:

| Species | Identity | Her IgE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dog (Can f 3) | **87.0%** | not tested |
| **Pig (Sus s 1)** | **79.4%** | **14.40** |
| **Cow (Bos d 6)** | **78.3%** | **2.38** |
| **Chicken (Gal d 5)** | **46.5%** | — |

Meaningful IgE cross-reactivity generally needs **>70% identity**, is sporadic between 50–70%, and is
uncommon below 50%. **Chicken sits at 46.5% — below the band.**

ThermoFisher's own Fel d 2 monograph puts it plainly: cross-reactivity from Fel d 2 to chicken serum
albumin "may be observed as **a rare event**."

**Revised: ~15–20%**, not "front of the queue." It rises steeply — toward 50% — if any of these is
true:

- She has ever kept or lived with a **bird** (parrot, budgie, chickens, aviary), or used **feather
  pillows / a down duvet**
- Her egg reactions are **yolk-selective** and **runny-selective**
- She also reacts to **chicken meat**

**Bird-egg syndrome runs bird → egg**, driven by inhaled avian albumin. Her exposure is cats. She
would need the weaker **mammal → bird** direction.

✅ **Still worth testing**, because Gal d 5 is **partially heat-labile** — IgE binding fell **88%
after 90 °C for 30 minutes** — which predicts the same raw-bad / cooked-fine pattern she already
shows with beef. If it is positive, the picture becomes almost elegant.

### 🛑 The ordering trap that would silently defeat this

**Egg white extract contains no yolk albumin.** Ordering "egg IgE" or an "egg white component
profile" **structurally cannot test this hypothesis.**

**Egg yolk must be ordered separately — Mayo YOLK, test 82753.**

## 2. The plants: pollen-food allergy syndrome is the leading answer

**Probability ~65–75%.**

Her leading symptom — **itchy mouth and throat first** — is the defining feature of pollen-food
allergy syndrome. The mechanism: she is sensitized to a **pollen** protein by breathing it, and that
IgE accidentally recognizes a look-alike protein in raw plant foods. The food was never the
sensitizer.

It is common: **13–58% of adults**, and **up to 70% of birch-pollen-allergic people.**

### ✅ The reassuring part

| Outcome | Frequency |
| --- | --- |
| Symptoms confined to the mouth | **Most cases** — stomach acid destroys the protein and ends the reaction |
| Systemic symptoms without oral symptoms | up to 3% |
| **Anaphylactic shock** | **1.7%** |

### ⭐ The single best history question in this document

A validated marker cluster. Reacting to **melon, watermelon, cucumber, zucchini, citrus, tomato,
banana, pineapple or persimmon** — or a combination — identifies **profilin** sensitization with
**85% sensitivity and >85% specificity.**

**Profilin** is present in nearly every plant, which is exactly why someone sensitized to it
"reacts to plants" in general. It is heat-labile and digestion-labile, and the **large majority of
profilin-allergic patients have mouth-only symptoms and tolerate the same foods cooked.**

Other patterns worth matching against:

| Cluster | Foods | Points to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Birch / Bet v 1** | apple, pear, cherry, peach, plum, almond, hazelnut, kiwi, carrot, celery | PR-10 group |
| **Grass** | celery, melon, orange, peach, tomato | — |
| **Ragweed** | banana, cucumber, melon, sunflower seed, zucchini | profilin |
| **Mugwort–celery–spice** | celery, carrot, parsley, dill, fennel, cumin, coriander | profilin |

### ⚠️ One nuance that breaks the simple rule

"Cooking always destroys these" is an over-simplification. Apple **Mal d 1** is destroyed
irreversibly — but peach **Pru p 1** and celery **Api g 1** can **refold after heating.** So a
cooked-food tolerance that holds for apple may not hold for peach or celery.

## 3. 🛑 The one branch where being wrong is dangerous

**LTP syndrome (Pru p 3 and relatives).** Probability here: **only 5–10%** — but test for it anyway,
because it is the only possibility on this page that could kill her.

**LTPs are heat-STABLE and digestion-stable. Cooking does not help.** A positive Pru p 3 is a red
flag, not a reassurance.

The severity distribution is almost the **inverse** of pollen-food syndrome. In a series of 236
LTP-allergic patients: **54.7% anaphylaxis, 36.0% hives/swelling, only 9.3% mouth-only.**

### How to tell them apart at the bedside

| | Pollen-food / profilin | **LTP syndrome** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw vs cooked | **Only raw.** Cooked is fine | **Both.** Cooking is irrelevant |
| Peeled vs unpeeled | Variable | **Peel much worse** — LTP concentrates there |
| Symptoms | Mouth, gone in minutes | **Systemic, anaphylaxis** |
| Cofactor needed | No | **Often — reacts only sometimes** |
| Pollen allergy | Nearly always | Often absent |
| Season | **Worse in pollen season** | No seasonal variation |

### Why she is probably not this

US LTP prevalence is low — "a minor allergen" here, versus 9–12% of allergy patients in Spain and
Italy. Her phenotype is oral-first. And **she does not drink alcohol**, which removes the single most
common cofactor and the commonest reason LTP reactions get missed. *(Exercise and NSAIDs are the
other two. Up to 30–40% of LTP reactions happen only with a cofactor present.)*

**Test: Pru p 3, ImmunoCAP f420.** Low odds, high consequence.

## 4. The lab artifact that could waste the whole plant workup

**CCD — cross-reactive carbohydrate determinants.** A sugar structure found on **plant and insect**
proteins that causes IgE tests to read positive across dozens of unrelated plant foods **with no
allergy behind any of them.**

- Found in roughly **35% of tested patients**, and **44% of those positive to more than one
  allergen** — which is exactly the pattern a plant panel would produce here.
- **It is clinically silent.** It explains positive *tests*, not *symptoms*.

Two things follow, and they point in opposite directions:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| ✅ **It cannot explain her meat results.** | Mammals do not make these sugar structures at all. Her pork / lamb / beef numbers are **genuine protein IgE** — the albumin conclusion is not undermined |
| ⚠️ **It could contaminate her plant results.** | And ordering *recombinant* components does not protect against it — interference has been documented in the test's own cellulose backbone |

**Run the CCD marker alongside the plant tests, not instead: MUXF3, Mayo test 609437.** If it is
positive, every weak plant positive is downgraded to an artifact — which could save her years of
unnecessary food avoidance.

## 5. The part that may not be allergy at all

Worth flagging honestly: **her downstream symptoms are not the classic pollen-food tail.**

Pollen-food syndrome that goes systemic produces **hives, swelling, GI or breathing trouble.** Hers
goes **dizziness → nausea → migraine.** That is a more **mast-cell and neurovascular** pattern — and
it sits alongside ten years of GI symptoms, headache and joint pain.

**Hereditary alpha-tryptasemia** is worth naming here: a common inherited trait, associated with
**female sex**, accounting for most elevated baseline tryptase seen in allergy practice, and linked
to more frequent and severe mast cell symptoms.

**Probability it is contributing: 25–35%** — as a **modifier**, not an alternative. And it has never
been tested. **One baseline tryptase settles whether it is even on the table.**

## 6. The test list

### Order together — first pass

| Test | Code | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Total IgE** | any lab | ⭐ **Order it first and read everything against it.** Never drawn |
| **Baseline tryptase** | any lab | Never drawn. >8 ng/mL → consider the tryptase trait |
| **MUXF3 (CCD marker)** | **Mayo 609437** | The interpretive guard for every plant result |
| **rBet v 2 — profilin** | ImmunoCAP **t216** · **Mayo BETV2** | The leading plant hypothesis |
| **rBet v 1** | ImmunoCAP **t215** | The birch / PR-10 axis |
| **Pru p 3 — LTP** | ImmunoCAP **f420** | Rules out the dangerous branch |
| **Egg white components** (Gal d 1 + Gal d 2) | **Mayo EGWTP 610706** | Ovomucoid = baked egg unsafe; ovalbumin only = baked egg often fine |
| **🛑 Egg YOLK** | **Mayo YOLK 82753** | The Gal d 5 proxy. **Omitting this defeats the whole egg question** |
| **Fel d 2 — cat albumin** | ImmunoCAP **e220** · Quest **93382** | Anchors the albumin cascade at its source |
| **Pollen: birch, grass, ragweed, mugwort** | extract IgE or skin test | **Without this, "pollen-food syndrome" is an assertion, not a diagnosis** |

### Only if indicated

**Chicken meat IgE** and **e219 chicken serum proteins** — only if egg yolk is positive and egg white
components are negative. That is the confirmation step for the avian albumin route.

**rPhl p 12** (grass profilin) — only if Bet v 2 is negative but the profilin food history is strong.
The two are cross-inhibitable; ordering both is usually wasted money.

### ⛔ Ordering traps

1. **Egg white does not contain Gal d 5.** Order egg yolk separately. *(Biggest trap here.)*
2. **Order total IgE with, not after.** Interpreting weak positives without it wastes the draw.
3. **Recombinant components do not exclude CCD.** Run MUXF3 in parallel.
4. **Do not order a broad food panel.** In a likely polysensitized patient this generates a field of
   weak positives that will take years to un-frighten her out of. Components only, hypothesis-driven.
5. **Do not re-test alpha-gal.** Two undetectable results a year apart is conclusive.
6. **Do not "clear" pork because she has never reacted to it.** She has never eaten it. At 79.4%
   identity to her likely sensitizer and an IgE of 14.40, it is **the highest-risk untested food in
   her diet.**

### On multiplex panels

**ALEX2 / ALEX3** would settle profilin, PR-10, LTP, CCD and the egg components in a single test —
and it is **research-use-only in the United States**, so it cannot be ordered clinically here. That
is precisely why the list above is long. (Available clinically in Europe, the UK and Canada.)

## 7. Questions worth more than the tests

**Raw versus cooked — the master question**

1. Take one food she reacts to. **Raw versus cooked — does cooking make it safe?** *(Carries more
   weight than any test on this page.)*
2. Canned, jarred, baked or juiced versions — apple sauce, tinned peaches, fruit pie?
3. **Peeled versus unpeeled?** Peel-dominant reactions point at LTP.

**Which plants**

4. ⭐ **Melon, watermelon, cucumber, zucchini, citrus, tomato, banana, pineapple, persimmon?**
5. Apple, pear, cherry, peach, plum, almond, hazelnut, kiwi, carrot, celery?
6. Peanut, tree nuts, peach skin, corn, mustard, lettuce, wheat?

**Season — decisive and almost never asked**

7. ⭐ **Are her food reactions worse during spring tree-pollen season, or grass and ragweed season?**
   Seasonal amplification is close to diagnostic for pollen-food syndrome. LTP does not vary.
8. **Does she have hay fever?** Itchy eyes, sneezing, blocked nose — and in which months? **No
   pollen allergy makes pollen-food syndrome very hard to sustain.**

**Egg**

9. **Yolk or white? Runny yolk versus hard-boiled?**
10. Mayonnaise, aioli, hollandaise, Caesar dressing, mousse, meringue, raw cake batter — versus a
    **well-baked cake or a hard-boiled egg**?
11. ⭐ **Has she ever kept or lived with a bird?** Feather pillows, down duvet?
12. **Chicken or turkey meat** — any reaction, and does cooking level matter?

**Cofactors**

13. Exercise within 2–4 hours of a reaction? **NSAIDs** — ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin?
14. Menstruating, unwell, sleep-deprived, fasting or acutely stressed? *(Alcohol already excluded.)*

**Timing**

15. **How many minutes from first bite to first symptom?** Pollen-food syndrome is seconds to
    minutes. Anything over an hour is a different mechanism.
16. **Every time, or only sometimes?** Intermittent reactions to an otherwise-tolerated food are the
    signature of **cofactor dependence** — which points at LTP.

## 8. What each result would change

| Finding | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| **Pru p 3 POSITIVE** | 🛑 **The biggest single change.** Epinephrine, formal cofactor counselling, and **cooking is not protective.** Turns a nuisance into a risk diagnosis |
| **Pru p 3 negative + profilin positive + pollen-allergic** | ✅ **Reassurance, and the largest quality-of-life gain available.** Cooked plant foods come back. Her diet re-expands |
| **MUXF3 positive** | Every weak plant positive downgraded to artifact. Prevents years of needless avoidance. Does **not** touch the meat results |
| **Total IgE >1000** | Weak positives reinterpreted. **Pork 14.40 survives this scrutiny** — it is too high to be noise at any plausible total. Beef 2.38 is more vulnerable, and conveniently beef is the one she tolerates well-done |
| **Ovomucoid positive** | **Baked egg is not safe.** Strict avoidance including baked goods |
| **Ovomucoid negative, ovalbumin positive** | Baked egg likely fine — a supervised baked-egg challenge becomes reasonable |
| **Egg yolk positive, egg white negative** | Points hard at **Gal d 5**. Confirm with chicken meat. Would make "one mechanism" almost true — and avoiding raw/runny yolk while keeping well-cooked egg becomes the rule |
| **Tryptase >8** | **Reframes the whole case.** The ten-year GI, headache and joint picture may be mast-cell rather than food-allergic — which opens treatments that food avoidance cannot provide |
| **Fel d 2 positive** | Converts a strong inference into a documented diagnosis, and raises cat avoidance or immunotherapy as an upstream option |
| **Everything negative despite a clear history** | Re-open non-IgE mechanisms. Consider supervised challenge. **Stop testing** |

## Confidence

**Well corroborated:** pollen-food prevalence and the pollen groupings · PR-10 and profilin
heat-lability · profilin sensitization at 10–30% of pollen-allergic patients · the profilin food
fingerprint and its 85% / >85% performance · LTP heat-stability and cofactor dependence (BSACI 2025
guideline) · CCD clinical irrelevance · Gal d 5 partial heat-lability (88% at 90 °C) · bird-egg
syndrome direction and route · ALEX research-use-only status in the US.

**Single-source — verify before quoting clinically:** the **46.5%** cat-to-chicken albumin identity
*(the direction is corroborated, the exact number is not)* · the 1.7% anaphylaxis figure in
pollen-food syndrome · the 35% / 44% CCD positivity rates · the 236-patient LTP series.

**Could not verify:** US prevalence of Pru p 3 sensitization *(the literature says "minor allergen"
without a number — do not let anyone quote one)* · US availability of a standalone Gal d 5 test
*(egg yolk extract is the workaround)* · US availability of ImmunoCAP ISAC.
